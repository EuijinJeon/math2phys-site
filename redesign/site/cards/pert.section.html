받는 것 (§5, §3, §2). 유계·순열 불변 상호작용과 유계 장(x:cavity-bounded), 고정 깊이 $L\ge2$ 와 지수 $m_d$(cavity:grid; 증명은 $0<m_1\le m_d$, $m_L=1$ 만 씀, 작성자 읽기), cavity:increment의 reservoir와 증분의 균등 유계성, cavity:negligible $|P_N^{L,\mathbf u}-NF_N|\le(\log2)s_N$ ($\mathbf u$ 에 균등), 섭동 틀(cavity:factor-bounds, x:cavity-root-convention: 개수·type·site는 root, $\mathbf u$ 는 고정값), $P_N^{L,\mathbf u}$(x:cavity-root-logarithm), $F_N$(model:hamiltonian). §3에서 power-mean recursion과 tilted kernel(tree:original-tilt, x:tree-path-kernels), tree 표본 규칙과 multioverlap(x:tree-sampling, tree:multioverlap), insertion calculus와 그 단조성(tree:Y-recursion, tree:insertion), 추가 변수의 약속(x:tree-extra-variables), canceled-anchor 전개와 그 해석적 경계(tree:extension-expansion, x:tree-extension-history).

내놓는 것. (1) pert:selection: 고정 $L\ge2$, $\varepsilon>0$ 마다 $N_j\uparrow\infty$ 와 결정적 $\mathbf u_j$ 가 있어 (i) $\Delta_{N_j}(\mathbf u_j)\le\underline F+2\varepsilon$, (ii) 모든 고정 type $\nu$ 에서 reservoir의 $e_{\nu,N_j}(\mathbf u_j)\to0$, (iii) reservoir의 깊이 $L$ 유한 prescribed tree 위 multioverlap 유한곱의 기대값이 수렴. (2) pert:tree-identity: 그런 모든 수열을 따라, 모든 고정 old tree $\mathcal T$, spin/marker anchor $a_*$, 유계 multioverlap 다항식 $f$, 유한 개 순서 붙은 추가 color, anchor와 추가 color들로 이루어진 집합 안의 쌍별 split 깊이의 일관된 제약 $\mathcal C$ 에 대해 $$\sum_{E\in\mathfrak E(\mathcal T,a_*,\mathcal C)}c_E\,\mathbb E[fR_E]-\mathbb Ef\sum_{E\in\mathfrak E(\{a_*\},a_*,\mathcal C)}c_E\,\mathbb E[R_E]\longrightarrow0,$$ 오차는 고정 $L$, tree, 제약에서 $N\to\infty$ 로 사라진다. 기대값은 선택된 벡터에서의 reservoir 기대값이다(x:pert-selected-limits).

물리 진술

x:pert-standing의 유계 모형에서, reservoir에 평균 $N^{3/4}$ 개의 유계 섭동 인자를 넣고, 크기와 섭동 매개변수를 골라(선택된 수열) cavity 증분을 낮게 유지하면서 모든 섭동 type의 score가 집중하게 한다. 그 집중을 새 섭동 하나의 insertion에 대한 Taylor 계수 항등식으로 바꾸고, 깊이별 mark로 갈라짐 깊이를 지정해 replica tree 위의 prescribed-tree 항등식을 얻는다.

차이

(1) 수열 선택은 존재 진술이고 매개변수 평균($\pi$)은 점을 고르는 데만 쓰인다. 결론은 고른 점에서의 root 와 기대값 표기 ($\mathbb E$, $E_{\mathcal T}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$)·path 기대값에 대한 것이다. (2) 모든 극한은 고정 $L$(과 $\varepsilon$)에서 $N\to\infty$ 이고 type, $L$, path 수에 균등하지 않다. §6 안에서는 $j$ 고정 $N\to\infty$ 뒤 $j\to\infty$ 다용어. (3) 항등식은 extension history 위의 부호 있는 합이고, 제약은 anchor와 추가 color들로 이루어진 집합 안의 쌍별 split 깊이(추가 color끼리의 쌍 포함)만 지정하고 다른 old replica가 관여하는 쌍은 지정하지 못한다. (4) 계층 구조는 보조 계층의 구성에 들어 있고 항등식에서 유도되지 않는다. 물리적 Gibbs 측도의 물리적 pure state 구조나 overlap 극한값은 나오지 않는다. (5) 원문이 드는 선행 연구와의 관계(perturbations.tex 17–22)는 prescribed-tree 항등식의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있다. 섭동 인자는 무한소가 아니라 $O(1)$ 크기이고 작은 것은 개수 밀도다.

물리 진술

x:pert-standing의 유계 모형과 그 reservoir에서, 섭동 매개변수를 그 구간들 위에서 균일하게 평균하면, 각 type의 score는 크기 $N\to\infty$ 에서 집중한다: 기대 개수로 정규화한 score의 path 요동과 root 와 기대값 표기 ($\mathbb E$, $E_{\mathcal T}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$) 요동이 함께 0으로 간다. 근거는 root logarithm이 $u_\nu$ 의 함수로서 (작은 보정을 더하면) 볼록이고, 그 2차 미분이 score의 path 분산을 포함한다는 것이다.

차이

(1) 매개변수 평균 $\int\cdot\,d\pi$ 의 진술이다. 특정 $\mathbf u$ 에서의 집중을 말하지 않는다. 한 점을 고르는 것은 pert:selection의 일이다. (2) type마다, $L$ 마다의 진술이고 상수는 $1/m_1=L$, $|I_\nu|$, $c_\nu$ 에 의존한다. $L$ 이나 type에 균등하지 않다. (3) 정규화된 평균 절대 편차($L^1$)의 수렴이며 disorder 표본 하나에 대한 거의 확실한 수렴이 아니다. (4) $N\to\infty$ 는 모든 크기를 따른 것이다(아직 선택된 수열이 없다). (5) $L\ge2$ 의 root logarithm은 보통 뜻의 free entropy가 아니고 path 기대값은 tilt된 보조 계층 위의 것이다.

물리 진술

root 와 기대값 표기 ($\mathbb E$, $E_{\mathcal T}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$)를 고정하면 root logarithm의 섭동 세기에 대한 2차 미분은 score의 path 분산(깊이별 가중 $m_d\ge m_1$)을 포함한다. 통계역학에서 free energy의 2차 미분이 요동을 주는 관계와 같은 꼴이다. 1차 미분이 score의 평균이라 $O(n)$ 으로 유계이므로, 2차 미분을 구간에서 적분한 것도 $O(\lambda_\nu)$ 이고, 정규화한 path 요동은 구간 평균으로 $O(\lambda_\nu^{-1/2})$ 이다.

차이

$L\ge2$ 이면 2차 미분은 보통의 Gibbs 분산이 아니라 보조 계층의 깊이별 martingale 차분 분산에 $m_d$ 를 곱한 합이다. 하계에 쓰는 것은 $m_d\ge m_1$ 뿐이고 상수는 $1/m_1$ 에 의존한다($m_d=d/L$ 이면 $L$). 결론은 $u_\nu$ 에 대한 구간 평균에서만 성립하고, 특정 $u$ 에서의 진술이 아니다. 이 부분은 score의 root 요동을 다루지 않는다(s3, s4).

무작위 함수 $G(u)=X_0(u)+2nu^2$ 는 $J_\nu$ 에서 볼록이고, $u\in J_\nu$ 와 다른 모든 매개변수에 균등하게 $$\operatorname{Var}(G(u))\le C_\nu N,\qquad|\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G'(u)|\le C_\nu\lambda_\nu.$$

물리 진술

root logarithm 자체의 root 와 기대값 표기 ($\mathbb E$, $E_{\mathcal T}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$) 요동(물리 disorder와 섭동 항들에 대한 분산)은 $O(N)$, 곧 표준편차로 $O(\sqrt N)$ 이다. root 변수 하나(상호작용 하나, 장 하나, 섭동 항 하나)를 바꾸면 root logarithm이 유계만큼만 바뀌고, 그런 변수의 개수가 $O(N)$ 이기 때문이다.

차이

이 상계는 함수 $G$ 의 값의 root 요동(표준편차 $O(\sqrt N)$)에 대한 것이고, 그 도함수, 곧 score의 root 평균 $X_0'=\langle D_\nu\rangle$ 의 요동에 대한 것이 아니다. 함수값의 요동이 작다는 것만으로는 도함수의 요동이 작다는 것이 나오지 않으므로, 도함수로 옮기려면 볼록성을 쓰는 s4가 필요하다. 규모로는 $\sqrt N$ 이 score 규모 $\lambda_\nu=c_\nu N^{3/4}$ 보다 작고, 이 여유 $\sqrt N\ll\lambda_\nu$ 가 s4에서 차분 간격 $\rho$ 를 고를 자리를 준다. 상수 $C_\nu$ 는 type에 의존한다. 분산은 $\mathbf u$ 를 고정한 root 분산이다.

차이

(1) 존재 진술이다: 크기의 부분수열 하나와 크기마다 매개변수 벡터 하나를 고른다. 모든 $N$ 이나 거의 모든 매개변수에서 성립한다고 말하지 않는다. (2) (ii)는 type마다의 수렴이고 type이나 $L$ 에 균등하지 않다. (3) (i)은 상한뿐이고 증분의 극한이나 $\lim F_N$ 의 존재를 말하지 않는다. (4) (iii)은 수렴만 주고 극한값을 식별하지 않는다(대각 부분수열). (5) 수열은 $L$ 과 $\varepsilon$ 마다 다르다. 물리 그림의 "무한소 섭동으로 generic한 계를 고른다"와의 관계는 선택된 수열 (선택된 섭동계)의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있다. 이 수열은 상태가 아니다.

물리 진술

선택된 수열을 따라 reservoir에서 다음이 성립한다. old prescribed tree의 anchor replica에 섭동 방향 $k$ 개를 넣으면 새 replica $k$ 개를 붙이는 모든 방법(extension history)에 대한 부호 있는 계수 합이 나온다. 그 합을 old tree의 overlap 시험함수 $f$ 와 곱해 평균한 것은, 같은 합을 anchor 하나만 있는 tree에서 계산한 것에 $\mathbb Ef$ 를 곱한 것과 같아진다. 깊이별 mark로 anchor와 새 replica들로 이루어진 집합 안의 쌍별 split 깊이(새 replica끼리의 쌍 포함)를, 서로 일관되는 한 임의로 지정할 수 있다(다른 old replica가 관여하는 쌍은 지정하지 못한다). 이것이 prescribed-tree 항등식이다.

차이

(1) 고정 $L$, tree, 제약, $f$ 마다 $N\to\infty$ 에서만 성립하고 오차율은 주지 않으며 어느 것에도 균등하지 않다용어. 집중의 근거는 섭동 score의 type별 집중이다. (2) 꼴은 history 위의 부호 있는 합이다: $c_E$ 는 old leaf 재사용이 $+1$, fresh path가 음수 $\gamma_\beta$ 다용어. (3) 제약은 anchor와 추가 color들로 이루어진 집합 $\{0,\ldots,k\}$ 안의 쌍별 split 깊이의 일관된 모임만 지정하고(추가 color끼리의 쌍도 포함, perturbations.tex 434–435, 495–496), 다른 old replica가 관여하는 관계는 지정하지 못한다. mark 깊이는 $1,\ldots,L-1$ 이다. (4) 계층(tree) 구조는 이 항등식에서 나오는 것이 아니라 보조 계층의 구성에 들어 있다. 물리적 Gibbs 측도의 물리적 pure state 구조나 overlap의 극한값·분포는 이것에서 나오지 않는다. (5) 원문이 선행 연구로 드는 것과의 관계(perturbations.tex 17–22)는 prescribed-tree 항등식의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있고, 원문은 이 항등식이 그것들과 같다거나 그것들에서 나온다고 하지 않는다.

1\section{Perturbations and identities on prescribed trees}
2\label{pert:section}
3
4Throughout this section the interaction and field are bounded, and the
5depth $L\geq2$ is fixed.  The arguments apply both to the $N$-spin system
6and to the reservoir in \cref{cavity:increment}. Its physical disorder
7consists of independent bounded interactions with a Poisson count of
8mean $O(N)$, together with $N$ independent bounded fields.
9All expectations in a statement at a specified parameter vector are
10taken with that vector held fixed.
11
12We choose perturbations whose derivatives concentrate, while selecting
13cavity increments at most an arbitrarily small error above the
14unperturbed liminf. A Poisson insertion
15then turns concentration into Taylor-coefficient identities. Independent
16signs along the inserted paths select the prescribed splitting depths.
17The use of concentration to obtain overlap identities has antecedents
18in the self-averaging argument of Ghirlanda--Guerra~\cite{GG98}.
19Related antecedents are the stochastic-stability relations of
20Aizenman--Contucci~\cite{AC98} and their extensions to diluted
21multioverlaps by Barra and De Sanctis~\cite{BarraDeSanctis07}.
22Panchenko's spin-distribution identities~\cite{Pan13} provide the cavity
23context. The bounded perturbation family and the identities on colored
24finite trees needed here are constructed and proved below.
25
26\subsection{The perturbation family}
27
28A \emph{mark specification} consists of a finite number $n\geq0$ of
29labeled Rademacher coordinates, a depth in $\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$ assigned
30to each coordinate, two functions
31$g,g_0:\{-1,1\}^n\to\mathbb Q\cap[-1,1]$, and a choice $S_0=1$ or
32$S_0=\sigma_i$. Each coordinate is sampled at its assigned depth along
33an auxiliary path. Distinct coordinates are independent; the value of
34a coordinate is shared at a shared vertex and is independent at
35different vertices. The case $n=0$ includes constant functions.
36We use all such specifications, a countable collection because each
37sign domain is finite. Products of signs and their rational averages
38belong to this collection; these will select branching patterns.
39
40The two parameters have different roles: differentiation in $u$ produces
41a concentrated score, while the values of $t$ probe an insertion's Taylor
42expansion. Taking $u$ smaller than every fixed power of $t$ will force
43each limiting Taylor coefficient to vanish. For each specification
44include types indexed by all integers $j\geq4$.
45The type $\nu$ corresponding to this specification and $j$ has
46\begin{equation}
47 t_\nu=j^{-1},\qquad
48 I_\nu=[j^{-j},2j^{-j}],\qquad u_\nu\in I_\nu.
49 \label{pert:parameters}
50\end{equation}
51Enumerate these types by $\nu\in\mathbb N$ and choose constants
52$c_\nu>0$ with $\sum_\nu c_\nu=1$.  Independently for each type, put
53$n_\nu\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(c_\nu s_N)$, where $s_N=N^{3/4}$.
54Each term has an independent uniform site $i\in[N]$ and an independent
55copy of its mark process.  At a leaf it multiplies the spin weight by
56\begin{equation}
57 \psi_\nu=1+t_\nu g\sigma_i+u_\nu g_0S_0.
58 \label{pert:factor}
59\end{equation}
60In particular $1/2<\psi_\nu<3/2$.  Counts, types, and sites belong to the
61root; the mark variables are sampled with their product priors at their
62assigned auxiliary depths.  Since the total count is
63$\operatorname{Poisson}(s_N)$, this construction uses only finitely many
64terms almost surely.  The factor bound permits termwise comparison
65through the power means, as in \eqref{cavity:negligible}.
66
67Write $\pi$ for the product of the uniform probability measures on the
68intervals $I_\nu$, and $\mathbf u=(u_\nu)_\nu$. We use $\pi$ to select
69deterministic parameter vectors, not as part of their quenched law.
70Each interval has fixed positive length for a fixed type; concentration
71estimates may therefore depend on its length.
72
73\subsection{Concentration of each perturbation score}
74
75For one type $\nu$, define on a tilted path, including its terminal
76Gibbs configuration,
77\begin{equation}
78 D_\nu=\sum_{q=1}^{n_\nu}
79 \frac{g_{0,q}S_{0,q}}
80 {1+t_\nu g_q\sigma_{i_q}+u_\nu g_{0,q}S_{0,q}}.
81 \label{pert:score}
82\end{equation}
83Let $\langle\cdot\rangle$ denote expectation along one tilted path
84conditional on the root, and let $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$ average the
85root at fixed $\mathbf u$.  Define
86\begin{equation}
87 e_{\nu,N}(\mathbf u)
88 =\frac{1}{c_\nu s_N}
89 \mathbb E_{\mathbf u}\Big\langle
90 \big|D_\nu-\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}\langle D_\nu\rangle\big|
91 \Big\rangle.
92 \label{pert:error}
93\end{equation}
94
95\begin{lemma}[Averaged score concentration]
96\label{pert:concentration}
97For each fixed $L$ and each fixed type $\nu$,
98\begin{equation}
99 \int e_{\nu,N}(\mathbf u)\,\pi(d\mathbf u)\longrightarrow0.
100 \label{pert:concentration-eq}
101\end{equation}
102The assertion holds for the reservoir as well as for the original
103system.  Constants need not be uniform in $L$ or $\nu$.
104\end{lemma}
105
106\begin{proof}
107Hold the other parameters fixed, write $u=u_\nu$, $n=n_\nu$, and put
108$\lambda_\nu=c_\nu s_N$.  Choose an open interval $J_\nu$ containing
109$I_\nu$ such that $|t_\nu|+|u|<1/2$ throughout its closure.  On this
110interval, pathwise,
111\begin{equation}
112 |D_\nu|\leq2n,\qquad
113 -4n\leq\partial_uD_\nu
114 =-\sum_{q=1}^{n}
115 \frac{(g_{0,q}S_{0,q})^2}{\psi_{\nu,q}^{2}}
116 \leq0.
117 \label{pert:score-bounds}
118\end{equation}
119
120\paragraph{Fluctuations along a tilted path.}
121Let $X_0(u)$ be the random root log partition function before taking the
122root expectation.  To include the last step in the differentiation,
123regard the logarithm of the spin weight as $X_L$ and the spin partition
124function as the step with exponent $m_L=1$.  Differentiation of one
125backward step gives
126\[
127 \partial_uX_{d-1}=K_d(\partial_uX_d),\qquad
128 \partial_u^2X_{d-1}
129 =K_d(\partial_u^2X_d)
130   +m_d\operatorname{Var}_{K_d}(\partial_uX_d).
131\]
132Thus, for $M_d=\langle D_\nu\mid\mathscr F_d\rangle$,
133\begin{align}
134 X_0'(u)&=\langle D_\nu\rangle,\label{pert:first-derivative}\\
135 X_0''(u)&=\langle\partial_uD_\nu\rangle
136   +\sum_{d=1}^{L}m_d
137        \langle(M_d-M_{d-1})^2\rangle \nonumber\\
138 &\geq m_1\operatorname{Var}(D_\nu\mid\mathscr F_0)-4n.
139 \label{pert:second-derivative}
140\end{align}
141Here the conditional expectations and variances use the tilted path
142law at the current $u$.  At fixed root all derivatives are justified by
143the bounded score and its derivatives; the finite recursion then
144justifies the displayed differentiation formulas.  Their root
145expectations are justified by the finite moments of the Poisson counts.
146
147Integrating \eqref{pert:second-derivative} over $I_\nu=[a,b]$ and using
148$|X_0'|\leq2n$ yields
149\begin{equation}
150 \int_a^b\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}
151       \operatorname{Var}(D_\nu\mid\mathscr F_0)\,du
152 \leq\frac{4\lambda_\nu(1+b-a)}{m_1}.
153 \label{pert:thermal-variance}
154\end{equation}
155Consequently the interval-averaged thermal mean absolute deviation,
156divided by $\lambda_\nu$, is at most
157\begin{equation}
158 \left(\frac{4(1+|I_\nu|)}
159 {m_1|I_\nu|\lambda_\nu}\right)^{1/2}.
160 \label{pert:thermal-bound}
161\end{equation}
162
163\paragraph{Fluctuations of the root mean.}
164It remains to concentrate $X_0'$ over the root. By
165\eqref{pert:second-derivative}, the random function
166\[
167 G(u)=X_0(u)+2nu^2
168\]
169is convex on $J_\nu$.  Uniformly for $u\in J_\nu$ and all the other
170parameters,
171\begin{equation}
172 \operatorname{Var}(G(u))\leq C_\nu N,
173 \qquad |\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G'(u)|\leq C_\nu\lambda_\nu.
174 \label{pert:root-variance}
175\end{equation}
176To prove the first estimate, condition on the physical and total
177perturbation counts. Conditional on the latter count, its types and sites
178are independent labels; changing a label replaces a single bounded
179factor. Apply the Efron--Stein variance inequality~\cite{EfronStein}
180$\Var Z\le\frac12\sum_j\E(Z-Z^{(j)})^2$, where $Z^{(j)}$ is obtained
181by independently resampling the $j$th input. For completeness, with
182independent inputs $X_1,\ldots,X_k$, let $E_j$ integrate only $X_j$ and
183let $\mathscr G_j=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_j)$. The Doob differences are
184$D_j=\E[Z-E_jZ\mid\mathscr G_j]$. Orthogonality and Jensen give
185\[
186 \Var Z=\sum_j\E D_j^2
187 \le\sum_j\E(Z-E_jZ)^2
188 =\frac12\sum_j\E(Z-Z^{(j)})^2.
189\]
190This independent-input decomposition requires neither identical laws
191nor a symmetric statistic; see Efron--Stein~\cite[Section~2]{EfronStein}.
192Resampling one interaction, field, or
193perturbation term changes $X_0$ by a bounded amount: this is immediate
194for the spin weights and is preserved by each power mean.  When a term
195is removed, its unused independent mark variables can be integrated
196out, so the same assertion holds for addition or deletion of a term.
197Conditional variances are therefore bounded by a constant times the
198number of root variables.  Conditional means are Lipschitz functions
199of the counts, with bounded Lipschitz constants.  If $Q,Q'$ are
200independent copies of a Poisson variable and $b$ is $C$-Lipschitz, then
201\[
202 \operatorname{Var}(b(Q))
203 =\tfrac12\mathbb E(b(Q)-b(Q'))^2
204 \leq C^2\operatorname{Var}(Q).
205\]
206Applying this to the independent counts completes the $O(N)$ estimate.
207The correction $2nu^2$ obeys the same estimates.  The derivative bound
208in \eqref{pert:root-variance} follows directly from
209\eqref{pert:score-bounds}.
210
211\paragraph{Passing from a convex function to its derivative.}
212Put
213$\bar G=\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G$ and $Z=G-\bar G$.  If $u\pm\rho$ lie
214in $J_\nu$, forward and backward difference quotients imply
215\begin{align*}
216 |G'(u)-\bar G'(u)|
217 &\leq\frac{|Z(u-\rho)|+2|Z(u)|+|Z(u+\rho)|}{\rho}\\
218 &\quad+\bar G'(u+\rho)-\bar G'(u-\rho).
219\end{align*}
220The monotonicity of $\bar G'$ gives
221\[
222 \int_a^b[\bar G'(u+\rho)-\bar G'(u-\rho)]\,du
223 \leq2\rho[\bar G'(b+\rho)-\bar G'(a-\rho)].
224\]
225Together with \eqref{pert:root-variance}, this proves
226\begin{equation}
227 \frac1{|I_\nu|}\int_{I_\nu}
228 \mathbb E_{\mathbf u}|G'(u)-\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G'(u)|\,du
229 \leq C_\nu\left(\frac{\sqrt N}{\rho}
230              +\frac{\rho\lambda_\nu}{|I_\nu|}\right).
231 \label{pert:convex-bound}
232\end{equation}
233For all sufficiently large $N$, take $\rho=N^{-1/8}$, which lies
234within the fixed neighborhood margin.  After division by
235$\lambda_\nu=c_\nu N^{3/4}$, both terms tend to zero.  Removing the
236correction to $G'$ costs at most
237$C_\nu\mathbb E|n-\lambda_\nu|/\lambda_\nu
238\leq C_\nu\lambda_\nu^{-1/2}$.  Thus the root contribution to
239\eqref{pert:error} tends to zero.  Combining it with
240\eqref{pert:thermal-bound} proves the result after integration over
241$u_\nu$.  All estimates are uniform in the other parameters, so their
242integration against the remaining product measure proves
243\eqref{pert:concentration-eq}.
244\end{proof}

246\subsection{One sequence for low increments and all identities}
247
248The parameter choices must satisfy concentration and a low cavity
249increment simultaneously. Let $\underline F=\liminf_NF_N$ over all
250system sizes for the bounded model. Recall that
251$P_N^{L,\mathbf u}$ denotes the root-averaged perturbed log partition
252function, and put
253$\Delta_N(\mathbf u)=P_{N+1}^{L,\mathbf u}-P_N^{L,\mathbf u}$.
254
255\begin{lemma}[Deterministic parameter selection]
256\label[lemma]{pert:selection}
257For every fixed $L\geq2$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there are sizes
258$N_j\uparrow\infty$ and deterministic vectors $\mathbf u_j$ such that
259\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
260 \item $\Delta_{N_j}(\mathbf u_j)\leq \underline F+2\varepsilon$;
261 \item for every fixed type $\nu$,
262 $e_{\nu,N_j}(\mathbf u_j)\longrightarrow0$ in the reservoir;
263 \item in the reservoir, the expectations of every finite product of multioverlaps on
264 every fixed finite prescribed tree of depth $L$ converge along this
265 sequence.
266\end{enumerate}
267\end{lemma}
268
269\begin{proof}
270\emph{Find low averaged increments.}
271The uniform perturbation bound \eqref{cavity:negligible} gives
272\[
273 \liminf_N\frac1N\int P_N^{L,\mathbf u}\,\pi(d\mathbf u)=\underline F.
274\]
275Hence infinitely many $N$ satisfy
276$\int\Delta_N\,d\pi\leq \underline F+\varepsilon$: otherwise telescoping the
277averaged increments would contradict this liminf.  \Cref{cavity:increment}
278supplies a constant $C$ such that
279$|\Delta_N(\mathbf u)|\leq C$ uniformly.
280
281\paragraph{Choose parameters with small score errors.}
282For a finite collection of types, Lemma~\ref{pert:concentration} and
283Markov's inequality show that the set where all its errors are small
284has parameter measure tending to one.  More explicitly, choose
285$N_j$ successively among the preceding infinite set so large that
286\[
287 \sum_{\nu\leq j}\int e_{\nu,N_j}(\mathbf u)\,\pi(d\mathbf u)
288 \leq j^{-3}.
289\]
290Then the set $G_j$ where $e_{\nu,N_j}\leq j^{-1}$ for all $\nu\leq j$
291has complement of measure $q_j\leq j^{-2}$.  Its conditional mean
292increment is at most
293\[
294 \frac{\underline F+\varepsilon+Cq_j}{1-q_j}
295 \leq \underline F+2\varepsilon
296\]
297for all sufficiently large $j$.  A vector in $G_j$ with increment at
298most $\underline F+2\varepsilon$ can therefore be chosen. Relabeling the tail
299proves (i) and (ii).
300
301\paragraph{Make all finite-tree moments converge.}
302At fixed $L$ the finite labeled tree
303shapes and finite products of their multioverlaps form a countable
304collection.  Each such product is bounded by one in absolute value.
305A diagonal subsequence gives (iii), without affecting (i) or (ii).
306\end{proof}
307
308From now on $L$ is fixed and $N\to\infty$ means convergence along a
309sequence from Lemma~\ref{pert:selection}. Expectations below are evaluated at
310the selected deterministic vector, whose dependence on $N$ is
311suppressed.  No uniformity in $L$, in the number of paths, or in a
312perturbation type is asserted for these limits.

314\subsection{Poisson insertion and analytic extraction}
315
316Fix an old labeled tree $\mathcal T$, a distinguished leaf $a_*$, and
317a bounded polynomial $f$ in multioverlaps of its old replicas.  The
318polynomial and its coefficients are fixed as $N$ and the insertion
319parameters vary. The
320root-averaged expectation on this tree is denoted simply by
321$\mathbb E f$.  The anchor marginal is always one tilted path.
322Therefore \eqref{pert:error} implies, for each fixed type $\nu$,
323\begin{equation}
324 \left|\frac{\mathbb E[fD_\nu^{a_*}]
325       -\mathbb E f\,\mathbb E\langle D_\nu\rangle}
326       {c_\nu s_N}\right|
327 \leq\|f\|_\infty e_{\nu,N}\longrightarrow0.
328 \label{pert:score-identity}
329\end{equation}
330
331For a fixed mark specification, introduce an independent new uniform
332site and an independent copy of its mark process, and set
333$A=1+tg\sigma_i+ug_0S_0$.  Let $Y$ be its insertion recursion from
334Lemma~\ref{tree:insertion}, and let $W_{\mathcal T}(A)$ be the updated-tree
335density \eqref{tree:density}. The expectations in the following display use
336the unmodified reservoir, extended by the independent insertion
337variables with their prior kernels:
338\begin{align}
339 J_{N,\mathcal T}(t,u)
340 &=\mathbb E\left[
341 f\,\frac{g_0^{a_*}S_0^{a_*}}{A^{a_*}}
342          W_{\mathcal T}(A)\right],\label{pert:palm-expression}\\
343 H_N(t,u)
344 &=J_{N,\mathcal T}(t,u)
345   -\mathbb E f\,J_{N,\{a_*\}}(t,u).
346 \label{pert:analytic-difference}
347\end{align}
348In the single-anchor expression $J_{N,\{a_*\}}$, the test is $1$.
349
350\begin{lemma}[Palm identity]
351\label{pert:palm}
352If $\nu$ is a type with the specified marks, then
353\begin{equation}
354 |H_N(t_\nu,u_\nu)|\leq\|f\|_\infty e_{\nu,N}.
355 \label{pert:palm-bound}
356\end{equation}
357For a fixed mark specification the function $H_N(t,u)$ is the same
358for all its types $j$: its base reservoir contains the full original
359Poisson families, including all types.
360\end{lemma}
361
362\begin{proof}
363Apply the Poisson add-one identity to the sum over type-$\nu$ terms
364in $D_\nu$.  After division by its mean $c_\nu s_N$, the remaining
365count is again Poisson with mean $c_\nu s_N$, and all the other counts
366are unchanged.  Thus the remaining model has exactly the original
367reservoir law.  The distinguished term becomes an independent new
368insertion with parameters $t_\nu,u_\nu$.  Its score is
369$g_0^{a_*}S_0^{a_*}/A^{a_*}$.  The updated tree density is
370\eqref{tree:density}, so the anchor factor $A^{a_*}$ cancels.
371This proves
372\[
373 \frac{\mathbb E[fD_\nu^{a_*}]}{c_\nu s_N}
374 =J_{N,\mathcal T}(t_\nu,u_\nu),\qquad
375 \frac{\mathbb E\langle D_\nu\rangle}{c_\nu s_N}
376 =J_{N,\{a_*\}}(t_\nu,u_\nu).
377\]
378Equation~\eqref{pert:palm-bound} now follows from
379\eqref{pert:score-identity}.  In particular no type has been deleted
380from the law of the base reservoir.  This proves the last assertion.
381\end{proof}
382
383\begin{lemma}[Vanishing insertion coefficients]
384\label[lemma]{pert:analytic}
385For every mark specification included in the perturbation family, old tree, anchor, and bounded
386overlap-polynomial test $f$, every Taylor coefficient at $t=0$ of
387$H_N(t,0)$ tends to zero along the selected sequence.
388\end{lemma}
389
390\begin{proof}
391Lemma~\ref{tree:extension-expansion} gives a complex neighborhood
392$|t|,|u|<\rho$ on which the functions in
393\eqref{pert:analytic-difference} are analytic and uniformly bounded
394in $N$ and in the selected parameter vector.  Shrinking $\rho$ gives
395a uniform bound on their $u$-derivatives as well.  These constants may
396depend on the fixed tree, $L$, and $\|f\|_\infty$, but not on the type
397index $j$ in the mark specification.
398
399Each Taylor coefficient of $H_N(t,0)$ is a finite linear combination
400of multioverlap moments and products of two such moments on prescribed trees.
401Their coefficients are determined by the fixed tree, exponents, mark law,
402and polynomial test, and are independent of $N$. Indeed the canceled
403anchor expansion supplies finitely many extensions at any fixed
404order; averaging the independent insertion site turns its spin
405product into a multioverlap.  These coefficients converge by
406Lemma~\ref{pert:selection}(iii).  The uniform analytic bound then
407implies, by the geometric bound on the Taylor tails, that
408$H_N(t,0)$ converges uniformly on each smaller disk to an analytic
409function $H(t)$.
410
411For every sufficiently large fixed $j$, the corresponding type and
412Lemma~\ref{pert:palm} give
413\[
414 \limsup_{N\to\infty}|H_N(j^{-1},0)|
415 \leq C\limsup_{N\to\infty}|u_{\nu,N}|
416 \leq2Cj^{-j}.
417\]
418Here the score error tends to zero with $j$ fixed, and the same
419function $H_N$ is used for every $j$, by Lemma~\ref{pert:palm}.
420Hence $|H(j^{-1})|\leq2Cj^{-j}$.  If the first nonzero Taylor
421coefficient of $H$ had degree $q$, then
422$|H(j^{-1})|\geq c j^{-q}$ for all sufficiently large $j$, a
423contradiction.  Thus $H$ vanishes identically near zero, proving the
424claim coefficient by coefficient.
425\end{proof}

427\subsection{Prescribing the colored tree}
428
429The anchor is called a \emph{spin anchor} when $S_0=\sigma_i$, and a
430\emph{marker anchor} when $S_0=1$.  Added colors are ordered
431$1,\ldots,k$; the anchor has color $0$.  They occupy distinct replica
432positions, none equal to the anchor, according to the canceled-anchor
433extension rule of Lemma~\ref{tree:extension-expansion}.  A constraint
434$\mathcal C$ prescribes any consistent collection of their pairwise
435split depths, involving only the added colors and the anchor.
436It imposes no extra restrictions involving the other old replicas.
437Let $\mathfrak E(\mathcal T,a_*,\mathcal C)$ be the allowed extension
438histories, with coefficients $c_E$ from that lemma.  For a history
439$E$, set
440\[
441 R_E=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
442 \begin{cases}
443 \displaystyle\sigma_i^{a_*}\prod_{j=1}^k\sigma_i^{a_j(E)},
444       &\text{spin anchor},\\[2pt]
445 \displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^k\sigma_i^{a_j(E)},
446       &\text{marker anchor}.
447 \end{cases}
448\]
449For $k=0$ these are respectively $R_{\{a_*\}}$ and $1$.
450
451\begin{theorem}[Identity on prescribed trees]
452\label{pert:tree-identity}
453\label{pert:identity}
454For the perturbation family defined above, along every sequence from
455Lemma~\ref{pert:selection},
456\begin{equation}
457 \sum_{E\in\mathfrak E(\mathcal T,a_*,\mathcal C)}
458       c_E\,\mathbb E[fR_E]
459 -\mathbb E f
460  \sum_{E\in\mathfrak E(\{a_*\},a_*,\mathcal C)}
461       c_E\,\mathbb E[R_E]
462 \longrightarrow0.
463 \label{pert:tree-identity-eq}
464\end{equation}
465This holds for every fixed old tree, either kind of anchor, every
466bounded polynomial $f$ in its multioverlaps, every finite number of
467ordered added colors, and every prescribed constraint $\mathcal C$.
468The empty addition is allowed.  The error is taken to zero at fixed
469$L$, fixed tree, and fixed constraint, before any limit in $L$.
470\end{theorem}
471
472\begin{proof}
473For $k=0$, the claim is the vanishing constant coefficient from
474\cref{pert:analytic}, using constant marks. For $k\ge1$, we explain
475how to extract distinct colored marks. Fix rational-valued functions
476$g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_k$, all bounded by one and constructed jointly from
477the same finite collection of auxiliary marks.  The mixed insertion
478uses $A=1+\sum_{j=1}^kz_jg_j\sigma_i$.  Its mixed derivative in all
479$z_j$ is the symmetric multilinear form obtained by polarizing the
480degree-$k$ coefficient for a single $g$.  More explicitly, if
481$Q_N(g)=k![t^k]H_N(t,0)$, then the mixed derivative is
482\[
483 \frac{k^k}{2^k k!}
484 \sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^k}
485    \left(\prod_{j=1}^k\epsilon_j\right)
486 Q_N\left(k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\epsilon_jg_j\right).
487\]
488Every argument of $Q_N$ is rational-valued and bounded by one, so each
489resulting pair $(g,g_0)$ belongs to the fixed mark family.  Lemma
490\ref{pert:analytic} makes the displayed mixed derivative tend to zero.
491The mixed derivative expansion is precisely the ordered extension
492rule: there is no additional factorial in its coefficients.
493
494It remains to arrange that the expectation over the marks selects
495exactly the extensions satisfying $\mathcal C$.  For a pair of colors
496$a,b\in\{0,\ldots,k\}$ and a depth $1\leq d\leq L-1$, the event
497that their split depth is at least $d$ is the event that they share
498their vertex at depth $d$.  Introduce a new independent Rademacher
499coordinate at that depth and give both $g_a$ and $g_b$ its factor.
500Its contribution to their joint mark expectation is $1$ if the
501vertex is shared and $0$ otherwise.  For a finite collection of such
502requirements, use different independent coordinates for the
503different requirements and define each $g_a$ as the product of its
504assigned factors.  Then
505\begin{equation}
506 \mathbb E_{\rm marks}
507       \left[g_0^{a_*}\prod_{j=1}^kg_j^{a_j(E)}\right]
508 =\prod_{(a,b,d)\ {\rm required}}
509       \mathbf1_{\{a,b\text{ share their depth-}d\text{ vertex}\}}.
510 \label{pert:mark-selector}
511\end{equation}
512All marks in this calculation are independent of the base reservoir.
513
514Let $H_d(a,b)$ denote the sharing indicator in
515\eqref{pert:mark-selector}, with $H_0=1$ and $H_L=0$ for distinct
516replica positions. These are positions in the sampled tree, even when
517their spin configurations happen to agree. Exact splitting at depth $d$ is expressed by
518$H_d-H_{d+1}$, for $0\leq d\leq L-1$.  Products and finite linear
519combinations therefore select any prescribed consistent colored
520shape or collection of allowed shapes. For each term of this finite
521linear combination, the functions $g_0,\ldots,g_k$ are products of signs
522and hence take values in $\{-1,1\}$. These functions and their
523polarization averages belong to the mark family fixed at the start of
524the section. Constant marks cover $k=0$.
525
526Finally apply the mixed canceled-anchor expansion to the difference
527\eqref{pert:analytic-difference}, average the marks using the
528selector, and average the independent uniform insertion site.  The
529latter average is exactly $R_E$.  Lemma~\ref{pert:analytic} and
530polarization make the coefficient difference tend to zero, yielding
531\eqref{pert:tree-identity-eq}.  Finite linear combinations extend the
532conclusion from overlap monomials to the stated tests.  The
533construction uses only marks through depth $L-1$: the extension rule
534never reuses the anchor or an already used color, so no test of
535coincidence at depth $L$ is required.
536\end{proof}

538\begin{remark}[Order of the choices]
539The mark specifications and their positive weights $c_\nu$ are fixed
540first, for a fixed $L$.  Parameter averaging then gives one selected
541sequence on which all types concentrate and all finite overlap
542moments converge.  The analytic argument takes $N\to\infty$ with
543each $j$ fixed before sending $j\to\infty$.  Only after the resulting
544prescribed-tree identities have been established is $L$ allowed to
545increase.  No concentration estimate uniform over the countable
546family is needed.
547\end{remark}
