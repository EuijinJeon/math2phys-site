수학 진술

받는 것. 고정 $L\ge1$, 지수 $0=m_0<m_1<\cdots<m_L=1$, 임의의 확률 kernel 열 $K_1,\ldots,K_L$ (root 분포 고정, x:tree-path-kernels)뿐이다. 모형의 가정(factorization, positivity, 짝수 $p$, 섭동)은 하나도 쓰지 않는다. model:chain은 시행 법칙의 $\eta_d$, $x$를 깊이 $d$, $r$의 변수로 읽는 대응(trees.tex 26–27)에만 나온다. tilted kernel은 임의의 leaf 분배함수와 prior kernel에서 만든다(tree:original-tilt).

내놓는 것. (1) insertion calculus (tree:insertion): tilted kernel에서 $A$를 곱하면 root log가 $\log Y_0$만큼 바뀌고, kernel은 $K_d^A=(Y_d/Y_{d-1})^{m_d}K_d$, tree 밀도는 $W_{\mathcal T}(A)=\prod_aA^a\prod_\beta Y_\beta^{\gamma_\beta}$, $\gamma_\beta=m_{d_\beta}-n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1}$. recursion은 단조·동차. (2) log 전개 (tree:log-expansion): $A=1+tD$, $|t|\|D\|_\infty<1$에서 $\log Y_0=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}t^k}{k}\mathbb E_{Q_k}E_{\mathcal T}\prod_{a=1}^kD^a$ (root 조건부), $|t|\|D\|_\infty\le c<1$이면 $K$차 뒤 나머지 $\le\sum_{k>K}c^k/k$. (3) canceled-anchor 전개 (tree:extension-expansion): $\partial_{t_1}\cdots\partial_{t_k}E_{\mathcal T}^{A_{\boldsymbol t}}[H/A_{\boldsymbol t}^{a_*}]|_{\boldsymbol t=0}=\sum_Ec_EE_{\mathcal T_E}[H\prod_jD_j^{a_j(E)}]$, 좌변은 복소 근방에서 kernel에 균등하게 해석적·유계. (4) reference 합 (tree:reference-total): 한 걸음 $m_d-jm_{d+1}$, 처방 모양 $S$ 전체 $\kappa(S)=\prod_{v\in\operatorname{Br}(S)}\prod_{j=1}^{a_v-1}(m_{d_v}-jm_{d_v+1})$, old tree에 무관. (5) charging (tree:charging, $m_d=d/L$): old branching 깊이가 아닌 지정 vertex $b$개면 $\sum|c_E|\le C_{q,k}\delta^b$, 그리고 $m_{d_v}\ge\eta$이면 $|J|^{-1}\sum|c_E|\le C_{q,k,\eta}$. (6) 깊이 분포 (tree:regular-shapes, tree:averaging-remark, $m_d=d/L$): $Q_k\{\text{not regular}\}\le C_k(\eta+L^{-1})$, 배정 하나의 확률 $\le C_kL^{-a}$, 지워도 regular. (7) tree 표본 규칙과 multioverlap (x:tree-sampling, tree:multioverlap).

물리 진술

power mean 안에 넣은 insertion의 효과를, 깊이 $L$ 보조 계층에서 상태를 공유하는 replica tree 위의 기대값으로 정확히 전개하는 미적분이다. 섭동 미분 한 번이 replica 하나를 더하고(기존 replica 재사용 계수 1, 새 갈라짐 계수 $\gamma_\beta$), log 전개의 tree 법칙은 $Q_k$이다. 지정한 갈라짐 구조에 대한 계수 합은 닫힌 꼴 $\kappa(S)$이고, 갈라짐 깊이를 old 갈라짐에서 떼면 절댓값 합도 작게 묶인다.

차이

유한 깊이와 임의의 확률 kernel 열에서 증명한 정확한 항등식과 부등식이다. $n\to0$ 해석적 연속도, 무한 계층도, Gibbs 측도의 물리적 pure state 분해도 쓰지 않는다(trees.tex 7–12). 물리 계산에서 이 자리를 차지하는 도구(power mean, $\gamma_\beta$, $Q_k$의 물리 이름)와의 대응은 power mean $T_d$, tree 계수 $\gamma_\beta$, $\kappa(S)$, tree 법칙 $Q_k$ 와 log 전개의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있고 증명에 쓰이지 않는다. 계수의 부호 있는 합은 큰 상쇄를 품는다(−δ 대 2+δ). 이것을 다루는 (5), (6)은 균등 격자 $m_d=d/L$에서만 진술된다. physics 칸은 해석적 경계, 수렴 영역, 상수를 뺐다.

물리 진술

kernel이 그 leaf 분배함수의 tilted kernel(tree:original-tilt)과 말단 Gibbs kernel일 때, spin의 Boltzmann 무게에 양의 함수 $A$(root가 주어진 조건부로 위아래 유계, tree:Y-recursion)를 곱하는 insertion은 power mean으로 반복한 root log를 정확히 $\log Y_0$만큼 바꾸고, 각 깊이의 kernel을 $(Y_d/Y_{d-1})^{m_d}$로 재가중한다. $L=1$이면 $\log Y_0=\log\langle A\rangle$이고 새 kernel은 $A$로 재가중한 Gibbs 측도다. replica tree의 법칙은 leaf마다 $A$, 내부 vertex마다 $Y_\beta^{\gamma_\beta}$를 곱한 만큼 바뀐다(계수 $\gamma_\beta$). recursion은 단조이고 동차다: root에만 의존하는 양의 $a\le A\le b$이면 모든 깊이에서 $a\le Y_d\le b$이고, $A$에 그런 상수를 곱하면 모든 $Y_d$가 같은 배가 된다.

차이

kernel이 그 leaf 분배함수의 power mean에서 나온 tilted kernel이어야 한다(임의의 kernel 열에 대한 $Y$ recursion은 정의되지만 "root log의 변화 $=\log Y_0$"는 tilted kernel에서만 뜻이 있다). $A$는 root가 주어진 조건부로 위아래로 유계여야 한다. $L\ge2$의 root log는 보통 뜻의 free entropy가 아니다용어.

수학 진술

유계 실수 path 함수 $D$와 $A=1+tD$에 대해 $$\log Y_0=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}t^k}{k}\mathbb E_{Q_k}E_{\mathcal T}\prod_{a=1}^kD^a,\qquad |t|\|D\|_\infty<1.$$ 이 항등식은 root가 주어진 조건부다. $|t|\|D\|_\infty\leq c<1$이면 차수 $K$ 뒤의 나머지의 절댓값은 $$\sum_{k>K}\frac{c^k}{k}$$ 이하다. (작성자 보탬, 원문 문맥에서 읽은 것: $Y_0$은 insertion 전 kernel로 계산한 (tree:Y-recursion), $\mathcal T$는 $Q_k$로 뽑은 tree, $E_{\mathcal T}$는 그 위의 원래 kernel 표본 기대값이다.)

물리 진술

유계 실수 path 함수 $D$에 대해, insertion $A=1+tD$의 효과 $\log Y_0$는 tree 법칙 $Q_k$로 뽑은 $k$개 replica tree 위에서 $D$의 곱의 평균을 계수 $(-1)^{k-1}t^k/k$로 더한 급수다($|t|\|D\|_\infty<1$에서, root 와 기대값 표기 ($\mathbb E$, $E_{\mathcal T}$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}$)가 주어진 조건부로; $|t|\|D\|_\infty\le c<1$이면 차수 $K$ 뒤 나머지의 절댓값은 $\sum_{k>K}c^k/k$ 이하). $L=1$이면 모든 replica가 독립 Gibbs 표본이라 $\mathbb E_{Q_k}E_{\mathcal T}\prod_aD^a=\langle D\rangle^k$이고, 식은 $\log(1+t\langle D\rangle)$의 Taylor 급수다. $L\ge2$에서는 $\langle D\rangle^k$ 자리에 계층 상태를 공유하는 replica들의 결합 평균이 들어간다.

차이

root가 주어진 조건부 항등식이고, 수렴 영역은 $|t|\|D\|_\infty<1$이다. 유한 깊이 $L$의 임의의 kernel 열에 대해 정확히 성립하며 $n\to0$ 연속을 쓰지 않는다. $Q_k$를 물리 그림의 replica overlap tree 법칙으로 읽는 것은 tree 법칙 $Q_k$ 와 log 전개의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있다.

새 replica를 지정한 reference replica들과 지정한 깊이에서 갈라지게 붙이는 모든 방법의 계수 합은, 갈라지는 깊이 $d$와 그 vertex에서 이미 reference가 차지한 가지 수 $j$만으로 $m_d-jm_{d+1}$이다. 기존 replica를 재사용하는 방법과 새로 갈라지는 방법이 서로 상쇄해서, old tree(이미 있던 replica들의 배치)는 합에서 사라진다. 지정된 갈라짐 구조 $S$ 전체의 합은 닫힌 꼴 $\kappa(S)$용어다.

차이

부호 있는 합에 대한 진술이다. 절댓값 합은 훨씬 클 수 있다(두 path 예에서 $-\delta$ 대 $2+\delta$, x:tree-cancellation-example). 제약은 reference와의 갈라짐 깊이만 지정한다. $\kappa(S)$를 replica 계산의 block counting으로 읽는 것은 tree 계수 $\gamma_\beta$, $\kappa(S)$의 물리 대응 칸에 intuition으로만 있다.

2
3A power-mean insertion admits two expansions.  The logarithmic expansion
4will compare pressures; an expansion with one canceled replica factor
5will extract the perturbation identities.  Both follow from one
6differentiation rule on trees.  We also bound their coefficients and the
7probability of exceptional branching depths. Power-mean calculations for
8hierarchical trial laws appear in M\'ezard--Parisi~\cite[Appendix~A]{MP01}
9and Panchenko--Talagrand~\cite[Section~5]{PT04}; the latter uses cascade
10weights introduced by Ruelle~\cite{Ruelle87}. Here we derive
11the needed identities directly for finite sequences of arbitrary
12probability kernels, without assuming an infinite hierarchy.
13
14\subsection{Path kernels and insertions}
15
16Fix an integer $L\geq1$ and numbers
17\[
18  0=m_0<m_1<\cdots<m_{L-1}<m_L=1.
19\]
20A path has depths $0,\ldots,L$.  Its depth-zero variable is the root.
21For $1\leq d\leq L$, the probability kernel $K_d$ draws the next variable
22given the whole prefix through depth $d-1$; $\mathscr F_d$ is the prefix
23sigma-field.  The root distribution is fixed throughout all insertions.
24In an $r$-level application, $L=r+1$: depths $1,\ldots,r$ carry
25auxiliary variables, and depth $L$ carries a Gibbs spin configuration.
26For the trial law \eqref{model:chain} with $r\ge1$, $\eta_d$ is the
27depth-$d$ variable for $0\le d<r$, and $x$ is the depth-$r$ variable.
28
29A finite tree $\mathcal T$ consists of a common root and finitely many
30labeled paths, each ending at depth $L$.  Every internal vertex has at
31least one child; in particular, all unary portions of paths are retained.
32At each vertex, different children are drawn independently using the
33appropriate kernel.  We write $E_{\mathcal T}$ for this sampling
34expectation conditional on the root.  A split at depth $d$ means that
35the last common vertex is at depth $d$.  Replica positions at depth $L$
36are distinct vertices, but the values sampled there need not differ.
37Thus independent spin replicas may be the same spin configuration.
38Removing some leaves and the portions of their paths unused by the
39remaining leaves preserves the sampling law of the remaining tree.
40
41When the terminal variables are spin configurations
42$\sigma^a\in\{-1,1\}^N$, the multioverlap on a subset $S$ of the
43replica leaves is
44\begin{equation}\label{tree:multioverlap}
45 R_S=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\prod_{a\in S}\sigma_i^a,
46 \qquad R_\varnothing=1.
47\end{equation}
48An unspecified expectation $\E$ below includes the root and the
49sampling of the indicated tree.
50
51Here is how the path kernels arise from a partition function. Let
52$P_d$, $1\le d<L$, be the auxiliary prior kernels, put
53$X_{L-1}=\log Z$, and recursively set
54\[
55 X_{d-1}=m_d^{-1}\log P_d e^{m_dX_d}\qquad(1\le d<L).
56\]
57The corresponding \emph{tilted kernels} are
58\begin{equation}\label{tree:original-tilt}
59 K_d(dz\mid\mathscr F_{d-1})
60 =e^{m_d(X_d(z)-X_{d-1})}P_d(dz\mid\mathscr F_{d-1}),
61 \qquad 1\le d<L.
62\end{equation}
63They are probability kernels by the recursion. The final kernel $K_L$
64is the Gibbs distribution of the spin configuration conditional on its
65auxiliary prefix. When $L=1$, only this final kernel is present.
66
67For a positive path function $A$, define
68\begin{equation}\label{tree:Y-recursion}
69  Y_L=A,\qquad
70  Y_{d-1}=\bigl(K_dY_d^{m_d}\bigr)^{1/m_d},
71  \qquad d=L,\ldots,1.
72\end{equation}
73We initially suppose that $A$ is bounded above and away from zero,
74conditionally on the root.  Every expression below is then well
75defined.  Denote the kernels after insertion by
76\begin{equation}\label{tree:updated-kernel}
77  K_d^A(dz\mid\mathscr F_{d-1})
78  =\left(\frac{Y_d(z)}{Y_{d-1}}\right)^{m_d}
79     K_d(dz\mid\mathscr F_{d-1}).
80\end{equation}
81The recursion shows that these are probability kernels.  Superscript
82$A$ on a tree expectation means that these kernels are used.
83
84\begin{lemma}[Insertion calculus]\label{tree:insertion}
85Suppose a leaf partition function is iterated by power means, with
86exponents $m_1,\ldots,m_{L-1}$, and $K_1,\ldots,K_{L-1}$ are its tilted
87kernels.  Let $K_L$ be its terminal Gibbs kernel.  Multiplying each spin
88weight by $A$ changes the iterated root logarithm by $\log Y_0$, where
89\eqref{tree:Y-recursion} uses the kernels before insertion.  The new
90kernels are \eqref{tree:updated-kernel}.
91
92For an internal vertex $\beta$ of a prescribed tree, let $d_\beta$ be
93its depth, let $n_\beta$ be its number of children, and put
94\[
95  \gamma_\beta=m_{d_\beta}-n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1}.
96\]
97The conditional joint density of its updated tree law is
98\begin{equation}\label{tree:density}
99  W_{\mathcal T}(A)
100  :=\frac{dP_{\mathcal T}^A}{dP_{\mathcal T}}
101  =\prod_{a\in\operatorname{Leaves}(\mathcal T)}A^a
102       \prod_{\beta\in\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal T)}
103            Y_\beta^{\gamma_\beta}.
104\end{equation}
105Here $Y_\beta$ is $Y_{d_\beta}$ evaluated on the prefix at $\beta$.
106
107The recursion is monotone and homogeneous: if $a\leq A\leq b$, for
108positive root-measurable $a,b$, then $a\leq Y_d\leq b$ at every depth;
109and multiplying $A$ by a positive root-measurable constant multiplies
110every $Y_d$ by that constant.
111\end{lemma}
112
113\begin{proof}
114At the terminal spin integration, the partition-function ratio is
115$K_LA=Y_{L-1}$, because $m_L=1$.  If $X_d$ and $X_d^A$ are the old and
116new backward logarithms, substitution in the preceding power mean
117gives
118\[
119  \exp\bigl(m_d(X_{d-1}^A-X_{d-1})\bigr)
120  =K_d\exp\bigl(m_d(X_d^A-X_d)\bigr).
121\]
122Backward induction proves $X_d^A-X_d=\log Y_d$ and then gives
123\eqref{tree:updated-kernel}; the same formula at $d=L$ is the usual
124Gibbs density ratio.
125
126Multiply the edge density ratios in \eqref{tree:updated-kernel} over
127the tree.  An internal vertex at positive depth $d$ receives exponent
128$m_d$ from its incoming edge and exponent $-n_\beta m_{d+1}$ from its
129outgoing edges.  At the root there is no incoming edge, which agrees
130with $m_0=0$.  Each terminal vertex receives exponent $m_L=1$.
131This proves \eqref{tree:density}.  Monotonicity and homogeneity follow
132at once by backward induction in \eqref{tree:Y-recursion}.
133\end{proof}
134
135An insertion may use additional variables that were absent from the
136original model.  Enlarge each path kernel by their independent prior
137kernels before applying the lemma.  The original backward logarithms
138do not depend on these variables, so the original tilt leaves their
139conditional prior kernels unchanged.  Additional root variables remain
140quenched.
141
142We will use differentiation of \eqref{tree:Y-recursion}.  For
143$A_{\boldsymbol t}=1+\sum_{j=1}^k t_jD_j$, where the real path functions
144$D_j$ are bounded, differentiation on any real neighborhood where
145$A_{\boldsymbol t}$ is uniformly positive gives
146\begin{equation}\label{tree:first-variation}
147  \partial_{t_j}\log Y_d
148  =E^{A_{\boldsymbol t}}\left[
149       \left.\frac{D_j}{A_{\boldsymbol t}}\right|\mathscr F_d\right].
150\end{equation}
151The conditional expectation on the right follows one new path below
152the specified prefix.  Indeed, differentiating one power mean gives
153$\partial_{t_j}\log Y_{d-1}=K_d^{A_{\boldsymbol t}}
154\partial_{t_j}\log Y_d$, and the terminal derivative is $D_j/A$.
155
156\paragraph{Analytic bounds.}
157The expressions are analytic on the complex domain
158\[
159 \sum_j|t_j|\|D_j\|_\infty<1,
160\]
161with bounds uniform over the kernels.
162On a smaller closed domain with this sum at most $\rho<1$, $A$ has argument bounded
163by $\vartheta=\arcsin\rho<\pi/2$ and modulus between $1-\rho$ and
164$1+\rho$.  If $Y_d$ has argument in $[-\vartheta,\vartheta]$, then
165$K_dY_d^{m_d}$ has argument in
166$[-m_d\vartheta,m_d\vartheta]$, using the principal powers.  It is
167nonzero, and taking its $1/m_d$ power gives the same argument bound
168for $Y_{d-1}$.  More quantitatively, a lower modulus bound $a_d>0$
169gives the lower bound
170$a_{d-1}=a_d\cos(m_d\vartheta)^{1/m_d}>0$, whereas the upper modulus
171bound $1+\rho$ is preserved.  These bounds justify integration of
172analytic functions at each step and all subsequent differentiations.
173They also bound \eqref{tree:density}, its canceled versions below,
174and their derivatives on smaller domains, uniformly over the kernels
175for fixed depth, exponents, tree size, and bounds on the inserted
176functions.

178\subsection{The two expansion rules}
179
180Call a leaf \emph{protected} if its factor $A^a$ in the tree density is
181canceled by a denominator $A^a$.  For a set $C$ of protected leaves and
182a bounded parameter-independent function $G$, consider
183$E_{\mathcal T}^A[G\prod_{a\in C}(A^a)^{-1}]$.
184Differentiating
185\eqref{tree:density} shows that
186\[
187 \partial_{t_j}\log W_{\mathcal T}(A)
188   =\sum_{a\in\operatorname{Leaves}(\mathcal T)}\frac{D_j^a}{A^a}
189      +\sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal T)}
190           \gamma_\beta\,\partial_{t_j}\log Y_\beta.
191\]
192In the derivative of
193$E_{\mathcal T}^A[G\prod_{a\in C}(A^a)^{-1}]$, the leaf terms at
194$a\in C$ cancel exactly with differentiation of their denominators.
195Each remaining leaf term inserts $D_j^a/A^a$ and makes that leaf
196protected.  By \eqref{tree:first-variation}, an internal-vertex term
197is the same expectation after adjoining an independent updated path
198below $\beta$, with an additional factor $D_j/A$ at its terminal
199vertex.  This is precisely a fresh path at $\beta$, with coefficient
200$\gamma_\beta$, and its new leaf is protected.  Conditional
201independence of children and the projectivity of tree sampling justify
202this representation even when $G$ uses the other children of $\beta$.
203
204For a tree with $n$ leaves, telescoping gives the useful identity
205\begin{equation}\label{tree:telescoping}
206  \sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal T)}
207       \bigl(n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1}-m_{d_\beta}\bigr)=n.
208\end{equation}
209All summands are positive.  Define a probability law $Q_k$ on trees
210with leaves labeled $1,\ldots,k$ as follows.  Start with a single path.
211When the current tree has $n$ leaves, choose an internal vertex
212$\beta$ with probability
213\begin{equation}\label{tree:Q-rule}
214  \frac{n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1}-m_{d_\beta}}{n},
215\end{equation}
216create a fresh child there, and continue with a completely new path
217to depth $L$.  Give its terminal vertex label $n+1$.  Identity
218\eqref{tree:telescoping} verifies the normalization.  The random tree
219is chosen independently of all variables subsequently sampled on it.
220For example, under $Q_2$ the two paths split at depth $d$ with probability
221$m_{d+1}-m_d$, for $0\le d<L$. This split is uniform on the depths when
222$m_d=d/L$.
223
224\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic expansion]\label[lemma]{tree:log-expansion}
225For a bounded real path function $D$ and $A=1+tD$,
226\begin{equation}\label{tree:log-series}
227  \log Y_0
228   =\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}t^k}{k}
229       \mathbb E_{Q_k}E_{\mathcal T}
230          \prod_{a=1}^k D^a,
231       \qquad |t|\|D\|_\infty<1.
232\end{equation}
233This identity is conditional on the root.  If
234$|t|\|D\|_\infty\leq c<1$, the absolute value of the remainder after
235order $K$ is at most
236\begin{equation}\label{tree:log-tail}
237  \sum_{k>K}\frac{c^k}{k}.
238\end{equation}
239\end{lemma}
240
241\begin{proof}
242The first derivative is, by
243\eqref{tree:first-variation}, the expectation of $D/A$ on one updated
244path.  Protect that leaf.  Every subsequent derivative can only add
245a fresh path, because all current leaves are protected.  At order
246$k$, all $k-1$ fresh coefficients are negative.  After normalization
247by \eqref{tree:telescoping}, their absolute products are
248$(k-1)!$ times the probabilities in $Q_k$.  Therefore
249\[
250  \left.\frac{d^k}{dt^k}\log Y_0\right|_{t=0}
251    =(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!\,
252       \mathbb E_{Q_k}E_{\mathcal T}\prod_{a=1}^kD^a.
253\]
254Analyticity proves \eqref{tree:log-series}; the coefficients have
255absolute value at most $\|D\|_\infty^k/k$, which proves the stated
256convergence domain and \eqref{tree:log-tail}.
257\end{proof}
258
259Here is the companion rule with old replicas.  Fix a tree
260$\mathcal T$ with an anchor leaf $a_*$.  Starting from all its old
261paths, assign colors $1,\ldots,k$ in that order.  For the next color,
262one may either
263\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
264  \item choose an unused old leaf other than $a_*$, with coefficient
265  $1$; or
266  \item create a fresh path from any internal vertex $\beta$ of the
267  current tree, with coefficient
268  $\gamma_\beta=m_{d_\beta}-n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1}$.
269\end{enumerate}
270Each fresh path uses a new child at its divergence vertex and new
271vertices at every greater depth.  Every chosen leaf receives its color
272and cannot be chosen again.  The current tree includes all old paths,
273including unused ones, and every path already added.  An extension
274history $E$ records these choices; write $a_j(E)$ for the leaf carrying
275color $j$, $\mathcal T_E$ for its final tree, and $c_E$ for the product
276of its coefficients.
277
278\begin{lemma}[Canceled-anchor expansion]\label{tree:extension-expansion}
279Let $H$ be a bounded function of the variables on the old tree, fixed
280as the insertion parameters vary.  For
281$A_{\boldsymbol t}=1+\sum_{j=1}^k t_jD_j$, with bounded real path
282functions $D_j$, one has
283\begin{equation}\label{tree:mixed-expansion}
284  \left.\partial_{t_1}\cdots\partial_{t_k}
285       E_{\mathcal T}^{A_{\boldsymbol t}}
286        \frac{H}{A_{\boldsymbol t}^{a_*}}
287  \right|_{\boldsymbol t=0}
288   =\sum_E c_E\,
289       E_{\mathcal T_E}
290          \left[H\prod_{j=1}^kD_j^{a_j(E)}\right].
291\end{equation}
292The formula holds in any fixed order of the colors.  For identical
293directions $D_j=D$, the coefficient of $t^k$ is the right-hand side
294divided by $k!$.  The analytic bounds established above apply to its
295left-hand side, uniformly over the path kernels.  The empty extension has weight $1$.
296\end{lemma}
297
298\begin{proof}
299Begin with $C=\{a_*\}$ and $G=H$.
300Apply the preceding rule successively, incorporating each new $D_j$
301in $G$.  Fresh leaves are protected immediately, so the only
302unprotected leaves are unused old leaves.  At zero all factors $A$
303and $Y$ are $1$, proving the claimed rule and its coefficients.
304\end{proof}

306\subsection{Sums of extension coefficients}
307
308We now control the signed normalization and the accumulated error in
309the prescribed-tree identities used for the lower bound.
310
311In an extension history, the anchor and the already colored paths
312are called reference paths.  Constraints on the new color will only
313specify its splitting depths with these paths.  In particular, a
314constraint does not distinguish unused old paths with the same
315specified ancestry.
316
317To see the cancellation that must be controlled, start with two old
318paths splitting at depth $d$, and let the first be the anchor. Require
319one added color to split from the anchor exactly at $d$. It may use the
320second old leaf, create a fresh path at the depth-$d$ vertex, or follow
321the second old path and leave it at a depth $\ell>d$. The respective
322coefficients give the total
323\[
324  1+(m_d-2m_{d+1})
325       +\sum_{\ell=d+1}^{L-1}(m_\ell-m_{\ell+1})
326  =m_d-m_{d+1}.
327\]
328On the uniform mesh $m_d=d/L$, with $\delta=L^{-1}$, this signed total
329is $-\delta$, but the sum of absolute coefficients is $2+\delta$.
330If instead the required split is delayed to $d+1<L$, the only allowed
331choice is a fresh split at the unary depth-$(d+1)$ vertex on the anchor
332path. Its coefficient is $-\delta$, so the absolute sum is now $\delta$.
333The next two lemmas extend these two calculations: the first
334computes signed totals, and the second controls absolute sums when
335specified splits occur away from old branching depths.
336
337\begin{lemma}[Reference-path totals]\label{tree:reference-total}
338Suppose the next color must follow a specified reference prefix to
339depth $d$ and then enter a child distinct from all $j\geq1$ children
340there that contain reference paths.  Apart from these ancestry
341constraints its continuation is unrestricted.  The sum of the
342coefficients of all allowed choices is
343\begin{equation}\label{tree:one-reference-total}
344  m_d-jm_{d+1}.
345\end{equation}
346Consequently, if the anchor and the colors must have a prescribed
347tree $S$, the total coefficient over all compatible extensions of
348any old tree is
349\begin{equation}\label{tree:K}
350 \kappa(S)=\prod_{v\in\operatorname{Br}(S)}
351           \prod_{j=1}^{a_v-1}(m_{d_v}-jm_{d_v+1}),
352\end{equation}
353where $a_v$ and $d_v$ are the child count and depth of each branching
354vertex in the prescribed reference tree.  More generally, the same
355one-step rule can be iterated starting from any already constructed
356set of references.
357\end{lemma}
358
359\begin{proof}
360At the specified divergence vertex, let $n$ be the child count in
361the entire current tree.  The $j$ excluded children contain all
362references passing through that vertex.  Each of the other $n-j$
363children roots a subtree containing no reference.  Its terminal
364vertices are therefore unused old leaves.  If such a subtree is
365rooted at depth $d+1$, its permitted choices are an old leaf, with
366coefficient $1$, or a fresh divergence at an internal vertex in the
367subtree.  If it has $q$ leaves, telescoping within it gives
368\[
369  q+\sum_{\beta\text{ internal in the subtree}}
370       (m_{d_\beta}-n_\beta m_{d_\beta+1})=m_{d+1}.
371\]
372The formula includes the case of a terminal child, when both sides
373are $1$.  Adding these contributions to the fresh choice at the
374specified vertex gives
375$m_d-nm_{d+1}+(n-j)m_{d+1}=m_d-jm_{d+1}$.
376
377For consistent prescribed splitting depths with all reference paths,
378the deepest required common prefix determines the divergence vertex
379and its excluded reference children.  Thus the one-step sum depends
380only on the current reference tree, not on which old paths or fresh
381paths realized it.  Sum backwards over successive choices.  At a
382branching vertex of the final prescribed tree, the numbers of
383excluded reference children when new child groups are first entered
384are exactly $1,\ldots,a_v-1$.  Their factors give
385\eqref{tree:K}, independently of the color order.
386\end{proof}
387
388To control errors after division by a small reference coefficient, we
389also need an absolute bound.  Moving a target branching depth away from
390all old branching depths forces a fresh unary split.  The next estimate
391charges one small factor to each such vertex.
392
393\begin{lemma}[Charging fresh splits]\label{tree:charging}
394Assume $m_d=d/L$ and put $\delta=L^{-1}$.  Start with an old tree having
395$q$ leaves and assign $k$ colors by the canceled-anchor extension
396rule.  Prescribe the reference shape of the anchor and the colors,
397possibly with additional restrictions on allowed extensions.  Suppose
398$b$ specified branching vertices of that shape have depths which
399are not branching depths anywhere in the old tree.  Then
400\begin{equation}\label{tree:charging-bound}
401   \sum_{E\text{ allowed}}|c_E|\leq C_{q,k}\delta^b.
402\end{equation}
403The constant is independent of $L$, the prescribed depths, and their
404coincidences at different vertices.
405
406In particular, for these $b$ vertices let
407\[
408 J=\prod_v\prod_{j=1}^{a_v-1}(m_{d_v}-jm_{d_v+1}).
409\]
410If $m_{d_v}\geq\eta>0$ at all of them, then $J\ne0$ and
411\begin{equation}\label{tree:relative-charging}
412   \frac{1}{|J|}\sum_{E\text{ allowed}}|c_E|
413        \leq C_{q,k,\eta}.
414\end{equation}
415\end{lemma}
416
417\begin{proof}
418If the current tree has $s$ leaves, \eqref{tree:telescoping} says
419that the total absolute coefficient of all fresh choices is $s$.
420There are at most $s$ eligible old choices.  Hence the total absolute
421coefficient at an unrestricted step is at most $2s\leq2(q+k)$.
422At any fixed depth there are at most $s$ vertices.  Restricting a
423step to fresh divergences at unary vertices of a prescribed depth
424therefore gives total absolute coefficient at most $s\delta$, because
425each such divergence has coefficient $m_d-m_{d+1}=-\delta$.
426
427Consider an allowed history and one of its specified target
428branching vertices.  That vertex cannot already be branching in the
429old tree.  At the first step at which it became branching in the
430current tree, a fresh path must therefore have diverged there while
431it was unary.  The vertex may itself have been created earlier as
432part of a new unary path; the same conclusion then applies.  Distinct
433target vertices become branching at distinct steps, since a fresh
434path creates only one branching vertex.  Assign each of the $b$
435vertices to its creation step.  There are at most
436$k!/(k-b)!$ possible assignments (and necessarily $b\leq k$).
437
438For each fixed assignment, enlarge the family of histories by
439discarding all constraints except that a designated step creates a
440unary split at its assigned depth.  Bound its step sum by
441$(q+k)\delta$ and every other step sum by $2(q+k)$.  Iterated summation
442over choices gives at most
443\[
444 \frac{k!}{(k-b)!}(q+k)^b\bigl(2(q+k)\bigr)^{k-b}\delta^b.
445\]
446This proves \eqref{tree:charging-bound}, including repeated assigned
447depths, because the designated steps remain distinct.
448
449For the final assertion, the $j=1$ factor at each designated vertex
450has absolute value $\delta$, whereas for $j\geq2$
451\[
452  |m_d-jm_{d+1}|=(j-1)m_d+j\delta\geq\eta.
453\]
454There are only a size-dependent number of the latter factors.  Thus
455$|J|\geq\delta^b\eta^{\sum_v(a_v-2)}$, and the result follows.
456\end{proof}

458\subsection{The distribution of branching depths}
459
460Still take $m_d=d/L$.  Suppress unary stretches only for the purpose
461of describing a shape by a finite topology and its branching-depth
462coordinates.  For $0<\eta<1/2$, call a shape \emph{regular} if every
463branching depth divided by $L$ lies in $(\eta,1-\eta)$ and any two
464distinct branching vertices have normalized depths differing by more
465than $\eta$.  A singleton is regular.  This is the regularity used
466when sampling a single tree; later we also use two copies whose
467corresponding depth assignments are deliberately aligned.
468
469\begin{lemma}[Regular shapes]\label[lemma]{tree:regular-shapes}
470For every fixed $k$ there is $C_k<\infty$, independent of $L$ and
471$\eta$, such that
472\begin{equation}\label{tree:irregular-probability}
473  Q_k\{\mathcal T\text{ is not regular}\}
474        \leq C_k(\eta+L^{-1}).
475\end{equation}
476For any particular labeled topology with $a$ branching vertices and
477any consistent assignment of their depths,
478\begin{equation}\label{tree:assignment-probability}
479  Q_k\{\text{that topology and depth assignment}\}
480       \leq C_kL^{-a}.
481\end{equation}
482Deleting leaves from a regular tree produces a regular tree, after
483discarding branching vertices that become unary.
484\end{lemma}
485
486\begin{proof}
487Suppose the current tree has $n$ leaves.  A new branching vertex is
488created exactly when the next path diverges at a unary vertex.
489Each such choice has probability $\delta/n$ by
490\eqref{tree:Q-rule}.  At any depth there are at most $n$ vertices;
491hence for a set $B$ of depths the probability that the next path
492creates a branching vertex at a depth in $B$ is at most $|B|\delta$.
493At each of the first $k-1$ steps, the boundary depths and the depths
494within normalized distance $\eta$ of any previous branching vertex
495form a set of cardinality at most $C_k(\eta L+1)$.  If none of these
496forbidden creation events occurs, the final tree is regular.
497A union bound proves \eqref{tree:irregular-probability}.  Divergence
498at an already branching vertex does not create a new coordinate and
499requires no extra exclusion.
500
501For \eqref{tree:assignment-probability}, restrict a specified final
502labeled tree to leaves $1,\ldots,n$, retaining every unary stretch.
503These restrictions determine its successive histories under
504\eqref{tree:Q-rule}.  Each of its $a$ branching vertices is first
505created by a unary divergence, whose probability is at most
506$\delta$; every other transition probability is at most $1$.
507The probability of the specified history is therefore at most
508$\delta^a$.  If a topology description identifies equivalent
509child orderings, their number is bounded in terms of $k$ alone,
510which gives the stated version with $C_k$.
511Finally, a retained branching vertex after deletion was already a
512branching vertex of the original tree, at the same depth.  Its depth
513coordinates are a subset of the old ones, so regularity is preserved.
514\end{proof}
515
516\begin{remark}\label{tree:averaging-remark}
517For fixed $k$ there are finitely many labeled topologies.  Therefore
518\eqref{tree:assignment-probability} converts an unnormalized depth
519average $L^{-a}\sum$ for a topology with $a$ branching coordinates
520into a bound for its contribution under $Q_k$.  The same is true for
521errors on subshapes: summing over coordinates discarded by the
522subshape costs at most the corresponding power of $L$.  Together
523with \eqref{tree:irregular-probability}, this allows averaged estimates
524on regular depth assignments to be used in the logarithmic expansion.
525\end{remark}
