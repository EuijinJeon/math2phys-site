(decor:overlap-concentration)

물리 진술

계층 깊이 $L$의 cavity reservoir에서 replica들이 어느 계층 깊이에서 같은 상태를 공유하는지(ultrametric tree의 모양과 갈라짐 깊이)를 정하면, 그 replica들의 모든 multioverlap은 self-average한다. 이것은 두 물리 가정의 합이다: 각 계층 상태 안에서 spin들은 clustering한다 (상태 안 요동 없음), 그리고 상태 평균 overlap은 상태와 disorder에 따라 요동하지 않는다.

차이

(1) 고정 깊이 배정마다의 진술이 아니라 $\eta$-regular 깊이 배정에 대한 평균 $\mathcal A_{L,\eta}$의 진술이다 (tex 11–12, 83 "Averaging over depths is essential"). 경계 근처 깊이와 서로 가까운 깊이는 제외된다. (2) 극한 순서는 고정 $L$에서 선택된 수열을 따라 $N\to\infty$, 그다음 $L\to\infty$이고 $N$에 대한 $L$-균등 속도는 없다 (decor:order-of-limits). (3) 대상은 perturbation을 넣은 reservoir의 보조 계층(tilted kernel의 prefix)이지 섭동 없는 Gibbs 측도의 pure state 분해가 아니다. (4) pair overlap만이 아니라 모든 유한 multioverlap에 대한 진술이다. 다만 극한값이 무엇인지, 깊이만의 함수 $q_d$로 수렴하는지는 말하지 않는다. 평균 주위로 집중한다는 것만 말한다.

물리 진술

상태 평균은 계층 깊이에 대해 평균적으로 연속이다. 깊이를 한 단계 옮겨도 상태 평균이 크게 바뀌는 깊이는 드물다. 계층을 따라 쌓이는 정보(martingale 증분의 에너지)가 전체로 유계이기 때문이다. Parisi 함수 $q(x)$가 단조 유계라서 큰 점프가 많을 수 없다는 그림과 같다.

1\section{Decorrelation on prescribed trees}
2\label{decor:section}
3
4Throughout this section $m_d=d/L$ and $\delta=L^{-1}$.  For each fixed
5$L$, all limits in $N$ are along the sizes and parameter choices supplied
6by \cref{pert:selection}; they satisfy the identities of
7\cref{pert:tree-identity}. Different values of $L$ may use different
8sequences. The argument uses only these identities and the tree sampling
9rules; in particular, it does not use the interaction factorization.
10
11Our goal is concentration of every finite multioverlap, averaged over
12separated interior branching depths. These are the averages needed for
13the comparison with independent messages in \cref{comp:section}.
14
15\subsection{Averages over depths}
16\label{decor:averages}
17
18A topology is a finite rooted tree on labeled leaves, with unary vertices
19suppressed.  Each branching vertex is assigned an integer depth in
20$\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$, increasing strictly along every branch.  Write $b(S)$
21for the number of branching vertices of a topology $S$.  Fix $\eta>0$.
22A depth assignment is \emph{$\eta$-regular} if every assigned depth divided
23by $L$ lies in $(\eta,1-\eta)$ and the normalized depths of distinct
24branching vertices differ by more than $\eta$.  When an additional
25prefix depth $d$ is specified, it must satisfy the same restrictions
26relative to all branching depths.  When two copies of one topology use
27aligned depths, corresponding vertices share one depth coordinate;
28regularity is imposed on these distinct coordinates.
29
30For a nonnegative array $z(\mathbf q)$ on $a$ depth coordinates, put
31\begin{equation}
32 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}z
33   =L^{-a}\sum_{\mathbf q\;\eta\text{-regular}}z(\mathbf q).
34 \label{decor:average-definition}
35\end{equation}
36Additional ordering restrictions, such as $d<\min_v q_v$, are included
37in the summation domain.  These are unnormalized averages: their total
38mass is at most one.  Consequently, for $0<\gamma\le1$,
39\begin{equation}
40 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}(z^\gamma)
41       \le (\mathcal A_{L,\eta}z)^\gamma.
42 \label{decor:average-holder}
43\end{equation}
44Two elementary comparisons will recur. Summing over an unused coordinate
45costs at most $L$, canceled by its normalization. A fixed integer shift of
46any subset of the coordinates maps $\eta$-regular assignments injectively
47into $\eta/2$-regular assignments for all sufficiently large $L$.
48
49There are only finitely many labeled topologies of any bounded size and,
50at fixed $L$, only finitely many assignments of their depths.  Thus
51errors in Theorem~\ref{pert:tree-identity} can be maximized over any
52of the finite collections used below before taking $N\to\infty$.
53We denote such a maximum by $\varepsilon_{N,L,k}$; for each fixed $L,k$,
54\begin{equation}
55 \varepsilon_{N,L,k}\longrightarrow0.
56 \label{decor:identity-error}
57\end{equation}
58Constants multiplying this error may depend arbitrarily on $L$.
59
60Recall that $R_S$ is the multioverlap of the sampled leaves of $S$,
61defined in \eqref{tree:multioverlap}. The result to be proved is the following.
62
63\begin{theorem}
64\label{decor:overlap-concentration}
65For every fixed labeled topology $S$,
66\begin{equation}
67 \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
68       \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\operatorname{Var}(R_S)=0
69       \qquad(\eta>0),
70 \label{decor:overlap-concentration-equation}
71\end{equation}
72where only the $b(S)$ branching depths are averaged. For a
73singleton, whose average has no coordinates,
74$\operatorname{Var}(R_S)\to0$ as $N\to\infty$ at every fixed $L$.
75\end{theorem}
76
77The proof has two parts. First we control the randomness below a
78sampled prefix through the squared conditional covariance of the
79spin-product vector. This is an induction on the number of leaves;
80delaying the target splits by one level will control its signed
81extension coefficients. We then use a marker anchor to concentrate the
82remaining conditional mean at each fixed depth. Averaging over depths
83is essential when combining the two estimates.
84
85\subsection{Conditional covariance and continuity in depth}
86
87For a sampled copy of $S$, set
88\[
89 A_S=\left(\prod_{a\in S}\sigma_i^a\right)_{i\le N},
90 \qquad R_S=\mathbf1\cdot A_S,
91 \qquad x\cdot y=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Nx_i y_i,
92 \qquad \|x\|_N^2=x\cdot x.
93\]
94At a depth $d$ on its initial stem, including its first branching depth,
95let $\mathscr F_d$ be generated by the root and the sampled prefix through $d$,
96and define
97\begin{align}
98 \mu_{d,S}&=\mathbb E[A_S\mid\mathscr F_d],\notag\\
99 v_N(S,d)&=\frac1{N^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i,j=1}^N
100  \operatorname{Cov}(A_{S,i},A_{S,j}\mid\mathscr F_d)^2.
101 \label{decor:covariance-definition}
102\end{align}
103The expectation includes the root. Every coordinate of $A_S$ and
104$\mu_{d,S}$ has absolute value at most one, and $0\le v_N(S,d)\le1$.
105The next lemma explains why this squared covariance is the useful
106quantity to induct on.
107
108\begin{lemma}[Contractions and three copies]
109\label{decor:contractions}
110If a random vector $B\in[-1,1]^N$ is conditionally independent of $A_S$
111given $\mathscr F_d$, then
112\begin{equation}
113 \bigl\|(A_S-\mu_{d,S})\cdot B\bigr\|_{L^2}
114        \le v_N(S,d)^{1/4}.
115 \label{decor:contraction-bound}
116\end{equation}
117If $X,Y,Z$ are conditionally independent copies of a bounded random
118vector given a sigma field $\mathscr G$, and $C$ is its conditional
119covariance matrix, then
120\begin{equation}
121 \mathbb E(X\cdot Y-X\cdot Z)^2
122       \ge \frac2{N^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}^2.
123 \label{decor:three-copy-bound}
124\end{equation}
125\end{lemma}
126
127\begin{proof}
128Conditional on $\mathscr F_d$ and $B$, the squared contraction has
129expectation $N^{-2}\sum_{ij}C_{ij}B_iB_j$.  Its absolute value is at
130most $N^{-2}\sum_{ij}|C_{ij}|$.  Cauchy--Schwarz, first over $i,j$
131and then over the prefix, proves \eqref{decor:contraction-bound}.
132For the second assertion, write $\mu=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathscr G]$.
133Conditional independence gives
134\[
135 \mathbb E[(X\cdot Y-X\cdot Z)^2\mid\mathscr G]
136 =\frac2{N^2}\operatorname{tr}\bigl((C+\mu\mu^{\mathsf T})C\bigr)
137 \ge\frac2{N^2}\operatorname{tr}(C^2),
138\]
139since $C$ is positive semidefinite.
140\end{proof}
141
142\begin{lemma}[Averaged continuity in depth]
143\label{decor:depth-continuity}
144Fix a topology $S$ and a subset $U$ of its branching vertices. For each
145$\eta$-regular depth assignment, let $S^U$ have the same topology with
146the depths of the vertices in $U$ increased by one. At an ancestral
147evaluation depth $d$, compare the means using the same sampled prefix.
148Then, for all sufficiently large $L$,
149\begin{equation}
150 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\mathbb E
151       \|\mu_{d,S^U}-\mu_{d,S}\|_N^2\le C/L.
152 \label{decor:branch-depth-continuity}
153\end{equation}
154The average ranges over all branching depths; the coordinate $d$ is
155also averaged if it ranges, and otherwise is held fixed. If $d$ ranges
156along an initial unary stretch of $S$, then
157\begin{equation}
158 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\mathbb E
159       \|\mu_{d+1,S}-\mu_{d,S}\|_N^2\le C/L,
160 \label{decor:evaluation-depth-continuity}
161\end{equation}
162where this average includes $d$ and all branching depths, and the means
163are evaluated along the same sampled path. The constant depends only on
164the number of vertices and shifts, uniformly in $N$, the evaluation
165depth, and the transition kernels.
166\end{lemma}
167
168\begin{proof}
169Keep the later branching depths of $T$ fixed and sample a single path
170along its initial stem. On this path, $\mu_{t,T}$ is a bounded vector
171martingale. Orthogonality of its increments gives
172\begin{equation}
173 \sum_t\mathbb E\|\mu_{t+1,T}-\mu_{t,T}\|_N^2\le1.
174 \label{decor:martingale-energy}
175\end{equation}
176This proves the evaluation-depth assertion after summing over the
177ranged depth and dividing by $L$.
178
179Consider a branching vertex at depth $q$ with child shapes
180$T_1,\ldots,T_s$.  Holding their later depths fixed, its original
181conditional mean is $\prod_j \mu_{q,T_j}$, with products understood
182coordinatewise.  If the split is delayed to $q+1$, its conditional mean
183at the same prefix of depth $q$ is
184$\mathbb E[\prod_j \mu_{q+1,T_j}\mid\mathscr F_q]$, where the child means
185are evaluated on a common one-step continuation.  Jensen's inequality
186and the product difference inequality on $[-1,1]^s$ yield
187\begin{equation}
188 \mathbb E\left\|\prod_j \mu_{q,T_j}
189       -\mathbb E\left[\prod_j \mu_{q+1,T_j}\mid\mathscr F_q\right]
190       \right\|_N^2
191 \le s\sum_j\mathbb E\|\mu_{q+1,T_j}-\mu_{q,T_j}\|_N^2.
192 \label{decor:one-vertex-shift}
193\end{equation}
194Summing over $q$ uses \eqref{decor:martingale-energy} with each child
195shape fixed.  Propagating a changed subtree mean towards an ancestral
196evaluation consists of conditional expectations and multiplication
197by factors bounded by one.  Both operations contract this squared
198norm.  Averaging over all other depth coordinates therefore proves
199the claimed $C/L$ bound for one shift.  For several shifts, change the
200vertices one at a time and use the squared triangle inequality with
201a factor equal to the number of shifts.  Regularity ensures that the
202intermediate shapes have the same ancestry relations and, for large
203$L$, lie in the enlarged $\eta/2$-regular domain.  All relevant
204one-prefix marginal laws are unchanged when other sampled branches
205are omitted, so these estimates hold in any larger sampled tree.
206\end{proof}
207
208Suppose now that $S$ first branches at depth $e$ into proper child shapes
209$T_1,\ldots,T_s$. Conditional independence of the children gives
210$\mu_{e,S}=\prod_j\mu_{e,T_j}$. Write
211\begin{equation}
212 a_{S,e}=\prod_{j=1}^s \mu_{e,T_j},
213 \qquad p_N(S,e)=\sum_{j=1}^s v_N(T_j,e)^{1/4}.
214 \label{decor:projection-definition}
215\end{equation}
216For any vector $B\in[-1,1]^N$ independent of the continuation of $S$
217given its prefix at $e$, successive replacement of the children,
218using \eqref{decor:contraction-bound}, gives
219\begin{equation}
220 \|(A_S-a_{S,e})\cdot B\|_{L^2}\le p_N(S,e).
221 \label{decor:projection-bound}
222\end{equation}
223At each replacement the other child vectors and the already replaced
224means form a bounded multiplier independent of the child being
225replaced, conditionally on $\mathscr F_e$.  In particular this applies
226to $B=\mathbf1$, and to a vector sampled on another side separated
227before $e$.  For two such sides, both can be projected with total
228$L^2$ error at most $2p_N(S,e)$.  Multiplication of the contracted
229error by any scalar test $f$ with $|f|\le1$, even a correlated one,
230does not increase its $L^1$ bound.

232\subsection{Conditional decorrelation}
233
234\begin{proposition}
235\label{decor:conditional-concentration}
236For every fixed labeled topology $S$ and every $\eta>0$,
237\begin{equation}
238 \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
239       \mathcal A_{L,\eta}v_N(S,d)=0,
240 \label{decor:conditional-concentration-equation}
241\end{equation}
242where the average ranges over all branching depths of $S$ and an
243additional regular depth $d$ strictly before its first split.  For a
244singleton, the average ranges over $d$ alone.
245\end{proposition}
246
247\begin{proof}
248We induct on the number $k$ of leaves. The induction replaces each
249proper child by its conditional mean. It then applies the prescribed-tree
250identity to two copies with delayed branching depths; after summing the
251signed coefficients, this becomes a nonnegative three-copy estimate.
252To keep track of the depth averages, let
253\begin{equation}
254 V_{k,L}(\eta)=\max_{1\le |S|\le k}
255       \limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal A_{L,\eta}v_N(S,d).
256 \label{decor:V-definition}
257\end{equation}
258The maximum is over finitely many labeled topologies.  Averages of
259any lower-order covariance used below, including averages with extra
260unused coordinates or shifted depths, are bounded by the appropriate
261$V_{k-1,L}(\eta/2)$, using
262\eqref{decor:average-holder} when fractional powers occur.
263
264\paragraph{One leaf.}
265Take two old paths splitting at $d$, with path $1$ a spin anchor, and
266add one color required to split from the anchor exactly at $d$.
267The available old path $2$ has coefficient $1$.  A fresh path can
268split from both old paths at $d$, with coefficient $-c_d$, or follow
269path $2$ and leave it at $\ell>d$, with coefficient $-c_\ell$, where
270\begin{equation}
271 c_d=2m_{d+1}-m_d=(d+2)\delta,
272 \qquad c_\ell=\delta\quad(d<\ell\le L-1).
273 \label{decor:c-weights}
274\end{equation}
275From the anchor alone the total coefficient is $-\delta$.
276Write $X=R_{12}$ and $X_\ell=R_{13}$ for the corresponding fresh
277extension.  Each $X_\ell$ has the marginal law of $X$, and
278$\sum_{\ell=d}^{L-1}c_\ell=1+\delta$.  The prescribed-tree identity
279with $f=X$ therefore gives
280\begin{equation}
281 \frac12\sum_{\ell=d}^{L-1}c_\ell
282      \mathbb E(X-X_\ell)^2
283       =\delta\operatorname{Var}(X)+o_N(1)
284       \le\delta+o_N(1).
285 \label{decor:singleton-identity}
286\end{equation}
287For $\ell=d$, the three spin vectors are conditionally independent
288below $d$.  Hence \eqref{decor:three-copy-bound} and $c_d\ge\eta$
289give $v_N(S,d)\le\delta/\eta+o_N(1)$ at each fixed $L,d$.
290This proves $V_{1,L}(\eta)\le\delta/\eta$.

292\paragraph{Shifted copies and their coefficients.}
293Let $|S|=k\ge2$, its first split be $e>d$, and its proper child shapes
294be $T_1,\ldots,T_s$.  Sample an old tree consisting of two copies of
295$S$, denoted I and II, whose stems split at $d$ and whose internal
296depth assignments are aligned.  Fix an old leaf in I as the spin
297anchor.  Let $S^+$ be the topology $S$ with every internal branching
298depth increased by one.  In the prescribed-tree identity require
299the anchor and the added colors to form two copies of $S^+$,
300separated at $d$, with the anchor in its original labeled position
301in the first copy.  These constraints involve only the anchor and
302the added colors.  Add all other colors of side I first, then those
303of side II. \Cref{decor:shift-figure} illustrates one local shift.

332Let $\kappa(T)$ be the reference coefficient \eqref{tree:K}, and put
333\begin{equation}
334 J=\kappa(S^+)^2.
335 \label{decor:J-definition}
336\end{equation}
337At each
338vertex the $j=1$ factor is $-\delta$; for $j\ge2$ its absolute value
339is at least $\eta/2$ for all sufficiently large $L$.  If $b=b(S)$,
340then
341\begin{equation}
342 |J|\ge c_{k,\eta}\delta^{2b}>0.
343 \label{decor:J-lower-bound}
344\end{equation}
345For large $L$, regularity ensures that each shifted depth differs from
346every branching depth of the old tree, including $d$. Thus these $2b$
347target branching vertices must be created by fresh unary splits.
348Lemma~\ref{tree:charging} gives
349\begin{equation}
350 \frac1{|J|}\sum_{\text{allowed extensions}}|c|
351        \le C_{k,\eta}.
352 \label{decor:relative-absolute-cost}
353\end{equation}
354The two copies have aligned depths but distinct branching vertices:
355each vertex supplies its own factor $\delta$. This is the reason for
356the shift. The full signed coefficient has an additional factor
357$-\delta$ from separation at $d$; we divide by $J$ and leave that
358factor in the identity. Equation~\eqref{decor:relative-absolute-cost}
359then controls every projection error after division.
360
361\paragraph{Projecting the two target sides.}
362In every extension, the marginal target tree consists of two
363ordinary copies of $S^+$, independent below their separation at $d$.
364By \eqref{decor:projection-bound} its multioverlap differs in
365$L^2$ from the contraction of its projected means at $e+1$ by at
366most $2p_N(S^+,e+1)$.  Change the proper child depths back by one,
367then change their evaluation depth from $e+1$ to $e$.  The product
368difference inequality and Lemma~\ref{decor:depth-continuity} show
369that this adds an error at most $h_N(S)$ in $L^2$, with
370\begin{equation}
371 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}h_N(S)^2\le C_k/L.
372 \label{decor:projection-shift-error}
373\end{equation}
374One can take $h_N(S)$ to be the sum of the finitely many
375$L^2(\|\cdot\|_N)$ distances in these comparisons, multiplied by
376a size-dependent constant.  This definition depends only on the
377single-copy marginal shapes, not on the particular extension.
378Thus \eqref{decor:projection-shift-error} holds uniformly for the
379comparisons in the extension sum, even though the union of the
380old and target trees itself need not have regular depths.
381
382Let $a,b$ denote the vectors $a_{S,e}$ on the old stems I and II.
383The target stem in I shares the old prefix through $e$ because it
384contains the anchor, so its final projected vector is $a$.
385
386\paragraph{The signed extension sum.}
387Set $f$ to be the overlap of all $2k$ old leaves, an allowed fixed
388overlap monomial. All target-I additions stay in the anchor's branch
389below $d$, so they leave the old-II stem and its child counts unchanged.
390Classify the first added path of target side II by its prefix
391through $e$.  It may diverge freshly at $\ell=d$, with coefficient
392$-c_d$, or follow the old II stem and diverge at $d<\ell<e$, with
393coefficient $-c_\ell=-\delta$.  In such a class let $b_\ell$ be
394$a_{S,e}$ on the new stem; it shares the old II prefix through
395$\ell$.  At $\ell=d$ the stems carrying $a,b,b_d$ are conditionally
396independent below $d$.  Every remaining possibility follows the
397old II prefix through $e$ and has final projected vector $b$.
398All later target paths in side II follow its first path through
399$e$, since their earliest split is $e+1$.
400
401For a fixed extension history, retain every old path and the first
402target-II prefix through $e$, and delete all other new branches.
403The target-I projection $a$ already depends only on the retained old
404anchor prefix. Each deleted subtree integrates to one under its
405probability kernels; a reused old leaf introduces no new variable.
406The retained tree depends only on the class: it is the old tree with a
407fresh stem attached at $\ell<e$, or just the old tree in the remaining
408class. The history is a combinatorial choice, not a condition on sampled
409values. Thus the joint marginal of the old paths and projected prefixes
410is fixed within each class. In particular, the expectation of $f$ times
411the projected overlap is constant within a class and can be multiplied
412by its total signed coefficient.
413
414By Lemma~\ref{tree:reference-total}, summing later side-II colors
415backwards gives $\kappa(S^+)$ for each first-path choice.  Summing the
416side-I choices gives the other factor $\kappa(S^+)$. The fresh class
417at $\ell$ therefore has total coefficient $-c_\ell J$.
418The total coefficient is $-\delta J$, both from the old tree and
419from the anchor alone. Since
420\[
421 \sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell=(e+1)\delta=m_e+\delta,
422\]
423the remaining prefix-through-$e$ class has coefficient $m_eJ$.
424Thus the projected terms have the following coefficients:
425\[
426 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
427 \text{side-II prefix} & \text{projected overlap}
428      & \text{total coefficient}\\ \hline
429 \text{fresh divergence at }d\le\ell<e & a\cdot b_\ell & -c_\ell J\\
430 \text{old prefix through }e & a\cdot b & m_eJ
431 \end{array}
432\]
433
434Multiply the projected overlap in each row by $f$ and divide the
435prescribed-tree identity by $J$. Rearranging gives
436\begin{equation}
437 \sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell
438   \mathbb E\bigl[f(a\cdot b-a\cdot b_\ell)\bigr]
439 =\delta\bigl(\mathbb E[f(a\cdot b)]
440          -\mathbb Ef\,\mathbb ER_{\mathrm{target}}\bigr)
441       +\rho_N(S,d),
442 \label{decor:induction-identity}
443\end{equation}
444where $R_{\mathrm{target}}$ on the right is the target overlap in
445the anchor-alone extension.  Its absolute value is at most one.
446The error satisfies
447\begin{equation}
448 |\rho_N(S,d)|\le C_{k,\eta}
449       \bigl(p_N(S^+,e+1)+h_N(S)\bigr)
450       +C_{L,k,\eta}\varepsilon_{N,L,k}.
451 \label{decor:induction-error}
452\end{equation}
453Indeed, multiplying a projection error by $f$, even when correlated
454with it, costs at most its $L^2$ norm since $|f|\le1$. Sum these errors
455with absolute coefficients and apply
456\eqref{decor:relative-absolute-cost}. The final term includes division
457of the original identity error by $J$ and tends to zero at fixed $L$.
458
459\paragraph{Closing the induction.}
460Equation~\eqref{decor:projection-bound} gives
461$\|f-a\cdot b\|_{L^2}\le2p_N(S,e)$.
462Moreover
463$\sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell=(e+1)\delta\le1$.
464Replacing $f$ by $a\cdot b$ on the left of
465\eqref{decor:induction-identity} therefore costs at most
466$4p_N(S,e)$.  Each $a\cdot b_\ell$ has the same marginal law as
467$a\cdot b$, so that left side becomes
468\[
469 \frac12\sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell
470        \mathbb E(a\cdot b-a\cdot b_\ell)^2.
471\]
472Its summands are nonnegative and $c_d\ge\eta$.  The right side
473of \eqref{decor:induction-identity} is bounded in absolute value
474by $2\delta+|\rho_N(S,d)|$.  Hence
475\begin{equation}
476 \mathbb E(a\cdot b-a\cdot b_d)^2
477 \le C_\eta\bigl(\delta+p_N(S,e)+|\rho_N(S,d)|\bigr).
478 \label{decor:projected-three-copy}
479\end{equation}
480Sample three copies of $A_S$ conditionally independently below
481$d$.  Projecting each of the two contractions involving the
482first copy changes it in $L^2$ by at most $2p_N(S,e)$.
483Using \eqref{decor:three-copy-bound},
484\eqref{decor:projected-three-copy}, and $p_N(S,e)\le k$, we obtain
485\begin{equation}
486 v_N(S,d)\le C_{k,\eta}\bigl(
487       \delta+p_N(S,e)+p_N(S^+,e+1)+h_N(S)\bigr)
488       +C_{L,k,\eta}\varepsilon_{N,L,k}.
489 \label{decor:covariance-induction-estimate}
490\end{equation}
491Each child in either $p_N$ has fewer than $k$ leaves. To make the
492averaging step explicit, the aligned construction has
493$D=b(S)+1$ distinct depth coordinates, including $d$ and $e$.
494A term belonging to $T_j$ uses only $D_j=b(T_j)+1$ coordinates.
495For the shifted term these are
496$\pi_j(\mathbf q)=(e+1,(q_v+1)_{v\in\operatorname{Br}(T_j)})$.
497Every retained assignment has at most $L^{D-D_j}$ preimages, and its
498coordinates are $\eta/2$-regular for large $L$. If $w_j$ is the child
499covariance as a function of these coordinates, then, for $0<\gamma\le1$,
500\begin{align}
501 L^{-D}\sum_{\mathbf q\;\eta\text{-regular}}
502       w_j(\pi_j(\mathbf q))^\gamma
503 &\le L^{-D_j}\sum_{\mathbf y\;\eta/2\text{-regular}}w_j(\mathbf y)^\gamma
504 \notag\\
505 &\le\left(L^{-D_j}\sum_{\mathbf y\;\eta/2\text{-regular}}
506       w_j(\mathbf y)\right)^\gamma.
507 \label{decor:fiber-bound}
508\end{align}
509The unshifted term has the same bound without translating the coordinates.
510Thus the two aligned copies use single-copy estimates; there is no
511restriction of an independently averaged pair of copies to a diagonal.
512Taking $\gamma=1/4$ and then $\limsup_N$, and using
513\eqref{decor:projection-shift-error} and
514\eqref{decor:identity-error}, gives, for sufficiently large $L$,
515\begin{equation}
516 V_{k,L}(\eta)\le C_{k,\eta}\left(
517       L^{-1/2}+V_{k-1,L}(\eta/2)^{1/4}\right).
518 \label{decor:induction-recursion}
519\end{equation}
520The maximum over smaller topologies in \eqref{decor:V-definition}
521can be absorbed in the right side because these covariances are
522bounded by one.  The base case and induction now imply
523$V_{k,L}(\eta)\to0$ for every fixed $k,\eta$, proving the proposition.
524\end{proof}

526\subsection{Concentration of every multioverlap}
527
528The covariance estimate controls fluctuations below a sampled prefix.
529We now use a marker anchor to concentrate the prefix projection, and
530combine this with the averaged continuity estimate.
531
532\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{decor:overlap-concentration}]
533For a singleton, use the spin-anchor identity with no added colors
534and $f=R_S$.  It gives
535$\mathbb ER_S^2=(\mathbb ER_S)^2+o_N(1)$.
536
537Suppose $S$ first splits at $e$ and has proper child shapes
538$T_1,\ldots,T_s$. We will condition one step before $e$. Use $S$ as
539the old tree, designate one of its
540leaves as a marker anchor, and add a full copy of $S$ all of whose
541leaves split from the anchor at $e-1$.  Its first path must be
542fresh at $e-1$, because the old tree has not yet branched there.
543All other target paths lie in the resulting fresh subtree.
544The extension coefficient on both sides of the prescribed-tree
545identity is the same nonzero number
546\begin{equation}
547 C(S,e)=-\delta \kappa(S).
548 \label{decor:marker-coefficient}
549\end{equation}
550The target and old copies are conditionally independent and
551identically distributed below $\mathscr F_{e-1}$. With
552$f=R_S$ and $M=\mathbb E[R_S\mid\mathscr F_{e-1}]$, their product has
553conditional expectation $M^2$. On the anchor-alone side, deleting the
554marker's continuation below $e-1$ integrates a probability kernel to one
555and leaves the ordinary target law $S$, with mean $\mathbb E R_S$.
556The identity therefore gives
557\begin{equation}
558 C(S,e)\bigl(\mathbb EM^2-(\mathbb E R_S)^2\bigr)
559 =C(S,e)\operatorname{Var}(M)=o_N(1),
560 \label{decor:marker-variance-identity}
561\end{equation}
562and hence
563\begin{equation}
564 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(M)\longrightarrow0
565 \quad\text{at each fixed }L,S,e.
566 \label{decor:prefix-projection-concentration}
567\end{equation}
568Division by $C(S,e)$ is made only at fixed $L$; no uniform lower
569bound as $L\to\infty$ is needed.
570
571It remains to control the conditional variance below this prefix.
572The child covariances are evaluated at $e$, which is separated from
573all their branching depths; the move to $e-1$ will use martingale
574continuity instead. Set
575\[
576 G=\mathbf1\cdot\prod_j \mu_{e,T_j},
577 \qquad G_-=\mathbf1\cdot\prod_j \mu_{e-1,T_j}.
578\]
579The latter is $\mathscr F_{e-1}$-measurable.  By
580\eqref{decor:projection-bound},
581$\mathbb E(R_S-G)^2\le p_N(S,e)^2$.
582Keeping all proper child depths fixed, the product difference
583inequality and \eqref{decor:martingale-energy} imply
584\begin{equation}
585 \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\mathbb E(G-G_-)^2\le C_k/L.
586 \label{decor:adjacent-prefix-error}
587\end{equation}
588Here $e$ is itself a ranged coordinate, so the one-step increments
589are summed before division by $L$.  Since conditional expectation
590is the optimal $L^2$ projection,
591\[
592 \mathbb E(R_S-M)^2
593   \le\mathbb E(R_S-G_-)^2
594   \le2p_N(S,e)^2+2\mathbb E(G-G_-)^2.
595\]
596Variance decomposition and
597\eqref{decor:prefix-projection-concentration} now show
598\begin{equation}
599 \limsup_{N\to\infty}
600      \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\operatorname{Var}(R_S)
601 \le C_k\left(L^{-1}+V_{k-1,L}(\eta/2)^{1/2}\right)
602 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }L.
603 \label{decor:variance-estimate}
604\end{equation}
605In this estimate we used
606$p_N(S,e)^2\le s\sum_jv_N(T_j,e)^{1/2}$ and
607\eqref{decor:fiber-bound} with $\gamma=1/2$: here the full average has
608$D=b(S)$ coordinates because $d$ is absent, while still
609$D_j=b(T_j)+1$. Proposition~\ref{decor:conditional-concentration}
610finishes the proof.
611\end{proof}

616identity errors.  At fixed $L$ there are finitely many relevant trees,

619uniform relative absolute-cost bound
620\eqref{decor:relative-absolute-cost}, the lower-order covariance
621averages, and the martingale estimate $C/L$.  No rate of convergence
622in $N$ uniform in $L$ is used.
