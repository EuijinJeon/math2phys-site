    §6 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 We choose perturbations whose derivatives concentrate, while selecting cavity increments at most an arbitrarily small error above the unperturbed liminf.

    s2 A Poisson insertion then turns concentration into Taylor-coefficient identities.

    s3 Independent signs along the inserted paths select the prescribed splitting depths.

    s4 The use of concentration to obtain overlap identities has antecedents in the self-averaging argument of Ghirlanda–Guerra [10].

    s5 Related antecedents are the stochastic-stability relations of Aizenman–Contucci [1] and their extensions to diluted multioverlaps by Barra and De Sanctis [3].

    s6 Panchenko's spin-distribution identities [14] provide the cavity context.

    s7 The bounded perturbation family and the identities on colored finite trees needed here are constructed and proved below.

    §6.1 The perturbation family¶

    §6.1 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 A mark specification consists of a finite number \(n\geq0\) of labeled Rademacher coordinates, a depth in \(\{1,\ldots,L-1\}\) assigned to each coordinate, two functions \(g,g_0:\{-1,1\}^n\to\mathbb Q\cap[-1,1]\), and a choice \(S_0=1\) or \(S_0=\sigma_i\).

    s2 Each coordinate is sampled at its assigned depth along an auxiliary path.

    s3 Distinct coordinates are independent; the value of a coordinate is shared at a shared vertex and is independent at different vertices.

    s4 The case \(n=0\) includes constant functions.

    s5 We use all such specifications, a countable collection because each sign domain is finite.

    s6 Products of signs and their rational averages belong to this collection; these will select branching patterns.

    §6.1 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 The two parameters have different roles: differentiation in \(u\) produces a concentrated score, while the values of \(t\) probe an insertion's Taylor expansion.

    s2 Taking \(u\) smaller than every fixed power of \(t\) will force each limiting Taylor coefficient to vanish.

    s3 For each specification include types indexed by all integers \(j\geq4\).

    s4 The type \(\nu\) corresponding to this specification and \(j\) has

    \[ t_\nu=j^{-1},\qquad I_\nu=[j^{-j},2j^{-j}],\qquad u_\nu\in I_\nu. \tag{6.1} \]

    s5 Enumerate these types by \(\nu\in\mathbb N\) and choose constants \(c_\nu\gt 0\) with \(\sum_\nu c_\nu=1\).

    s6 Independently for each type, put \(n_\nu\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(c_\nu s_N)\), where \(s_N=N^{3/4}\).

    s7 Each term has an independent uniform site \(i\in[N]\) and an independent copy of its mark process.

    s8 At a leaf it multiplies the spin weight by

    \[ \psi_\nu=1+t_\nu g\sigma_i+u_\nu g_0S_0. \tag{6.2} \]

    s9 In particular \(1/2\lt \psi_\nu\lt 3/2\).

    s10 Counts, types, and sites belong to the root; the mark variables are sampled with their product priors at their assigned auxiliary depths.

    s11 Since the total count is \(\operatorname{Poisson}(s_N)\), this construction uses only finitely many terms almost surely.

    s12 The factor bound permits termwise comparison through the power means, as in (5.3).

    §6.2 Concentration of each perturbation score¶

    §6.2 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 For one type \(\nu\), define on a tilted path, including its terminal Gibbs configuration,

    \[ D_\nu=\sum_{q=1}^{n_\nu} \frac{g_{0,q}S_{0,q}} {1+t_\nu g_q\sigma_{i_q}+u_\nu g_{0,q}S_{0,q}}. \tag{6.3} \]

    §6.2 ¶4¶

    원문

    s1 Fluctuations along a tilted path.

    s2 Let \(X_0(u)\) be the random root log partition function before taking the root expectation.

    s3 To include the last step in the differentiation, regard the logarithm of the spin weight as \(X_L\) and the spin partition function as the step with exponent \(m_L=1\).

    \[ \partial_uX_{d-1}=K_d(\partial_uX_d),\qquad \partial_u^2X_{d-1} =K_d(\partial_u^2X_d) +m_d\operatorname{Var}_{K_d}(\partial_uX_d). \]

    s5 Thus, for \(M_d=\langle D_\nu\mid\mathscr F_d\rangle\),

    s6 Here the conditional expectations and variances use the tilted path law at the current \(u\).

    s7 At fixed root all derivatives are justified by the bounded score and its derivatives; the finite recursion then justifies the displayed differentiation formulas.

    s8 Their root expectations are justified by the finite moments of the Poisson counts.

    §6.2 ¶6¶

    원문

    s1 Fluctuations of the root mean.

    s2 It remains to concentrate \(X_0'\) over the root.

    s3 By (6.8), the random function

    \[ G(u)=X_0(u)+2nu^2 \]

    ↳ is convex on \(J_\nu\).

    s4 Uniformly for \(u\in J_\nu\) and all the other parameters,

    \[ \operatorname{Var}(G(u))\leq C_\nu N, \qquad |\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G'(u)|\leq C_\nu\lambda_\nu. \tag{6.11} \]

    s5 To prove the first estimate, condition on the physical and total perturbation counts.

    s6 Conditional on the latter count, its types and sites are independent labels; changing a label replaces a single bounded factor.

    s7 Apply the Efron–Stein variance inequality [8] \(\operatorname{Var} Z\le\frac12\sum_j\mathbb E(Z-Z^{(j)})^2\), where \(Z^{(j)}\) is obtained by independently resampling the \(j\)th input.

    s8 For completeness, with independent inputs \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), let \(E_j\) integrate only \(X_j\) and let \(\mathscr G_j=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_j)\).

    s9 The Doob differences are \(D_j=\mathbb E[Z-E_jZ\mid\mathscr G_j]\).

    s10 Orthogonality and Jensen give

    \[ \operatorname{Var} Z=\sum_j\mathbb E D_j^2 \le\sum_j\mathbb E(Z-E_jZ)^2 =\frac12\sum_j\mathbb E(Z-Z^{(j)})^2. \]

    s11 This independent-input decomposition requires neither identical laws nor a symmetric statistic; see Efron–Stein [8, Section 2].

    s12 Resampling one interaction, field, or perturbation term changes \(X_0\) by a bounded amount: this is immediate for the spin weights and is preserved by each power mean.

    s13 When a term is removed, its unused independent mark variables can be integrated out, so the same assertion holds for addition or deletion of a term.

    s14 Conditional variances are therefore bounded by a constant times the number of root variables.

    s15 Conditional means are Lipschitz functions of the counts, with bounded Lipschitz constants.

    s16 If \(Q,Q'\) are independent copies of a Poisson variable and \(b\) is \(C\)-Lipschitz, then

    \[ \operatorname{Var}(b(Q)) =\tfrac12\mathbb E(b(Q)-b(Q'))^2 \leq C^2\operatorname{Var}(Q). \]

    s17 Applying this to the independent counts completes the \(O(N)\) estimate.

    s18 The correction \(2nu^2\) obeys the same estimates.

    s19 The derivative bound in (6.11) follows directly from (6.6).

    §6.2 ¶7¶

    원문

    s1 Passing from a convex function to its derivative.

    s2 Put \(\bar G=\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G\) and \(Z=G-\bar G\).

    s3 If \(u\pm\rho\) lie in \(J_\nu\), forward and backward difference quotients imply

    \[ \begin{align*} |G'(u)-\bar G'(u)| &\leq\frac{|Z(u-\rho)|+2|Z(u)|+|Z(u+\rho)|}{\rho} \\ &\quad+\bar G'(u+\rho)-\bar G'(u-\rho). \end{align*} \]

    s4 The monotonicity of \(\bar G'\) gives

    \[ \int_a^b[\bar G'(u+\rho)-\bar G'(u-\rho)]\,du \leq2\rho[\bar G'(b+\rho)-\bar G'(a-\rho)]. \]

    s5 Together with (6.11), this proves

    \[ \frac1{|I_\nu|}\int_{I_\nu} \mathbb E_{\mathbf u}|G'(u)-\mathbb E_{\mathbf u}G'(u)|\,du \leq C_\nu\left(\frac{\sqrt N}{\rho} +\frac{\rho\lambda_\nu}{|I_\nu|}\right). \tag{6.12} \]

    s6 For all sufficiently large \(N\), take \(\rho=N^{-1/8}\), which lies within the fixed neighborhood margin.

    s7 After division by \(\lambda_\nu=c_\nu N^{3/4}\), both terms tend to zero.

    s8 Removing the correction to \(G'\) costs at most \(C_\nu\mathbb E|n-\lambda_\nu|/\lambda_\nu \leq C_\nu\lambda_\nu^{-1/2}\).

    s9 Thus the root contribution to (6.4) tends to zero.

    s10 Combining it with (6.10) proves the result after integration over \(u_\nu\).

    s11 All estimates are uniform in the other parameters, so their integration against the remaining product measure proves (6.5).

    §6.5 Prescribing the colored tree¶

    §6.5 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 The anchor is called a spin anchor when \(S_0=\sigma_i\), and a marker anchor when \(S_0=1\).

    s2 Added colors are ordered \(1,\ldots,k\); the anchor has color \(0\).

    s3 They occupy distinct replica positions, none equal to the anchor, according to the canceled-anchor extension rule of Lemma 3.3.

    s4 A constraint \(\mathcal C\) prescribes any consistent collection of their pairwise split depths, involving only the added colors and the anchor.

    s5 It imposes no extra restrictions involving the other old replicas.

    s6 Let \(\mathfrak E(\mathcal T,a_*,\mathcal C)\) be the allowed extension histories, with coefficients \(c_E\) from that lemma.

    s7 For a history \(E\), set

    \[ R_E=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N \begin{cases} \displaystyle\sigma_i^{a_*}\prod_{j=1}^k\sigma_i^{a_j(E)}, &\text{spin anchor},\\[2pt] \displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^k\sigma_i^{a_j(E)}, &\text{marker anchor}. \end{cases} \]

    s8 For \(k=0\) these are respectively \(R_{\{a_*\}}\) and \(1\).

    ▸ Proof.¶

    §6.5 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 For \(k=0\), the claim is the vanishing constant coefficient from Lemma 6.4, using constant marks.

    s2 For \(k\ge1\), we explain how to extract distinct colored marks.

    s3 Fix rational-valued functions \(g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_k\), all bounded by one and constructed jointly from the same finite collection of auxiliary marks.

    s4 The mixed insertion uses \(A=1+\sum_{j=1}^kz_jg_j\sigma_i\).

    s5 Its mixed derivative in all \(z_j\) is the symmetric multilinear form obtained by polarizing the degree-\(k\) coefficient for a single \(g\).

    s6 More explicitly, if \(Q_N(g)=k![t^k]H_N(t,0)\), then the mixed derivative is

    \[ \frac{k^k}{2^k k!} \sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^k} \left(\prod_{j=1}^k\epsilon_j\right) Q_N\left(k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\epsilon_jg_j\right). \]

    s7 Every argument of \(Q_N\) is rational-valued and bounded by one, so each resulting pair \((g,g_0)\) belongs to the fixed mark family.

    s8 Lemma 6.4 makes the displayed mixed derivative tend to zero.

    s9 The mixed derivative expansion is precisely the ordered extension rule: there is no additional factorial in its coefficients.

    s1 It remains to arrange that the expectation over the marks selects exactly the extensions satisfying \(\mathcal C\).

    s2 For a pair of colors \(a,b\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\) and a depth \(1\leq d\leq L-1\), the event that their split depth is at least \(d\) is the event that they share their vertex at depth \(d\).
