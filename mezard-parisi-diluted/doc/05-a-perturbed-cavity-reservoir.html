    §5 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 We compare systems of sizes \(N\) and \(N+1\) through a common \(N\)-spin system, the reservoir.

    s2 This cavity comparison follows the Aizenman–Sims–Starr variational scheme [2] and its spin-distribution form in Panchenko [14, Section 2.2].

    s3 We add a sublinear number of bounded perturbations to obtain identities for its spin trees.

    s4 The comparison below shows that these perturbations change the pressure by \(o(1)\) and preserve the site-minus-bond cavity increment.

    §5 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 Fix \(L\ge2\) and use \(r=L-1\) auxiliary levels with

    \[ m_d=d/L\quad(0\le d\le L),\qquad\delta=1/L. \tag{5.1} \]

    s2 Let \((c_\nu)_{\nu\ge1}\) be positive with \(\sum_\nu c_\nu=1\), and let \(s_N=N^{3/4}\).

    s3 To the spin weight add independent Poisson families of single-site factors \(\psi_\nu\), with counts of means \(c_\nu s_N\) and independent uniform sites in \([N]\).

    s4 Each factor uses its own auxiliary variables, drawn along the hierarchy with product priors, and satisfies

    \[ 1/2\le\psi_\nu\le3/2. \tag{5.2} \]

    s5 The total number of factors is \(\operatorname{Poisson}(s_N)\) and is finite almost surely.

    s6 Counts, types, and sites are root variables.

    s7 The parameters of the factors are denoted by \(\mathbf u\) and are fixed, not quenched random variables, unless a parameter average is explicitly taken.

    s8 The factors needed later are specified in Section 6.

    ▸ Lemma 5.1 (Cavity increments)¶

    §5 ¶5¶

    원문

    s1 The increments \(\Delta_N^{L,\mathbf u}=P_{N+1}^{L,\mathbf u}-P_N^{L,\mathbf u}\) are uniformly bounded in \(N,L,\mathbf u\).

    s2 There is a reservoir on \(N\) spins with physical interaction count of mean

    \[ \rho_N=\alpha(N+1)\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^p, \tag{5.4} \]

    ↳ the usual fields, and the perturbation counts of means \(c_\nu s_N\), such that

    \[ \Delta_N^{L,\mathbf u} =\log2+\mathbb E\log Y_{0,\rm s}-\mathbb E\log Y_{0,\rm b}+o_N(1). \tag{5.5} \]

    s3 The error is uniform in \(L,\mathbf u\).

    s4 The insertion recursion uses the tilted reservoir kernels and independent new root disorder, with terminal insertions

    \[ \begin{align*} A_{\rm s}(\sigma)&=\frac12\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm1} \exp\left(h\varepsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{K_{\rm s}} \theta_j(\sigma_{i_{1,j}},\ldots,\sigma_{i_{p-1,j}},\varepsilon)\right), \tag{5.6} \\ A_{\rm b}(\sigma)&=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{K_{\rm b}} \theta_j(\sigma_{i_{1,j}},\ldots,\sigma_{i_{p,j}})\right), \tag{5.7} \end{align*} \]

    ↳ where \(K_{\rm s}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\alpha p)\), \(K_{\rm b}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\alpha(p-1))\), and all displayed indices are independent uniform indices in \([N]\).

    ▸ Proof.¶

    §5 ¶6¶

    원문

    s1 The physical interactions.

    s2 Split the Poisson interaction process of the \((N+1)\)-spin system according to the number of indices equal to \(N+1\).

    s3 Terms with no new index have mean \(\rho_N\).

    s4 Terms with exactly one new index have mean \(\alpha p(N/(N+1))^{p-1}=\alpha p+O(N^{-1})\); by permutation invariance we may place the new input last.

    s5 Their other indices are independent uniform indices in \([N]\), and their interaction functions retain the prescribed law.

    s6 Terms with at least two new indices have total mean \(O(N^{-1})\).

    s7 Meanwhile, the \(N\)-spin system can be obtained from the same reservoir by adding a Poisson number of old interactions of mean

    \[ \alpha N-\rho_N=\alpha(p-1)+O(N^{-1}). \]

    s8 These quantities are nonnegative for all sufficiently large \(N\).

    §5 ¶7¶

    원문

    s1 The perturbations.

    s2 The old-site perturbation count in the \((N+1)\)-spin system has total mean \(s_{N+1}N/(N+1)\), and the new-site count has mean \(s_{N+1}/(N+1)\).

    s3 Couple these processes to the reservoir perturbations using common Poisson points.

    s4 The expected number of unmatched factors is at most

    \[ |s_{N+1}-s_N|+2s_{N+1}/(N+1)=O(N^{-1/4}). \]

    s5 Deleting or adding a perturbation changes the root logarithm by at most \(\log2\); an interaction changes it by at most \(B\).

    s6 An unused auxiliary variable can simply be integrated out with its prior, so these comparisons remain valid when the number of auxiliary variables changes.

    §5 ¶8¶

    원문

    s1 The increment.

    s2 We may consequently discard the repeated-new-index interactions, match the perturbations, and replace the two insertion count means by their limits at a total expected cost \(O(N^{-1/4})\).

    s3 Summing over the new spin gives \(2A_{\rm s}\), while the old-system correction gives \(A_{\rm b}\).

    s4 The factor \(2\) supplies the term \(\log2\) in the increment.

    s5 The insertion identity for the power means then proves (5.5).

    s6 Finally,

    \[ |\log Y_{0,\rm s}|\le H+BK_{\rm s},\qquad |\log Y_{0,\rm b}|\le BK_{\rm b}. \]

    s7 Together with the coupling estimate this bounds the increments for large \(N\); increasing the constant covers the remaining sizes.

    s8 All estimates are uniform in \(L\) and \(\mathbf u\).
