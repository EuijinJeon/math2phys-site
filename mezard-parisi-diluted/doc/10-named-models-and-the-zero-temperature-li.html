    ▸ Corollary 10.1 (Viana–Bray and diluted even-spin models)¶

    §10 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 Fix an even integer \(p\ge2\), \(\alpha\gt 0\), and independent real random variables \(J,H\) such that \(J\) has a symmetric distribution and \(\mathbb E|J|+\mathbb E|H|\lt \infty\).

    s2 For every \(\beta\gt 0\), the model with

    \[ \theta_\beta(s_1,\ldots,s_p)=\beta J\prod_{\ell=1}^p s_\ell, \qquad h_\beta=\beta H \]

    ↳ satisfies the variational formula (2.13), with the trial functional evaluated at these interaction and field laws.

    s3 In particular, this holds for the Viana–Bray model [18], which is the case \(p=2\).

    §10 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 Use

    \[ a=\cosh(\beta J),\qquad b=\tanh(\beta J),\qquad f_\ell(s)=s. \]

    s2 The functions \(f_\ell\) are deterministic independent copies, and \(|b|\lt 1\) almost surely.

    s3 Symmetry makes every odd moment of \(b\) zero; the even moments are nonnegative.

    s4 Finally, \(B(\theta_\beta)=\beta|J|\) and \(|h_\beta|=\beta|H|\).

    s5 Thus all hypotheses of Theorem 2.1 hold.

    ▸ Corollary 10.2 (Soft random even-\(K\) SAT)¶

    §10 ¶4¶

    원문

    s1 Fix an even integer \(K\ge2\) and \(\alpha\gt 0\).

    s2 Let \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_K\) be independent fair signs and let \(W\ge0\) be independent of the signs, with \(\mathbb E W\lt \infty\).

    s3 For every \(\beta\gt 0\), the interaction

    \[ \theta_\beta(s_1,\ldots,s_K) =-\beta W\prod_{\ell=1}^K\frac{1+\varepsilon_\ell s_\ell}{2}, \qquad h=0, \]

    ↳ satisfies (2.13).

    s4 The choice \(W=1\) is the soft random even-\(K\) SAT model; \(W\) gives an independent nonnegative clause weight.

    §10 ¶5¶

    원문

    s1 The product \(I_\varepsilon(s)=\prod_\ell(1+\varepsilon_\ell s_\ell)/2\) takes values in \(\{0,1\}\) and indicates a violated clause.

    s2 Therefore

    \[ e^{\theta_\beta(s)} =1+(e^{-\beta W}-1)I_\varepsilon(s). \]

    s3 Take \(a=1\), \(b=e^{-\beta W}-1\), and \(f_\ell(s)=(1+\varepsilon_\ell s)/2\).

    s4 The functions are independent copies and independent of \(b\).

    s5 Since \(0\le-b\lt 1\), the strict product condition and every positivity inequality hold.

    s6 Moreover, \(B(\theta_\beta)=\beta W\), so Theorem 2.1 applies.

    §10 ¶6¶

    원문

    s1 For the spin model, define the unscaled energy score by

    \[ \mathcal E_N(\sigma) =\sum_{k=1}^{M_N}J_k\prod_{\ell=1}^p\sigma_{i_{\ell,k}} +\sum_{i=1}^N H_i\sigma_i. \]

    s2 For the satisfiability model, put

    \[ \mathcal E_N(\sigma) =-\sum_{k=1}^{M_N}W_k \prod_{\ell=1}^K\frac{1+\varepsilon_{\ell,k} \sigma_{i_{\ell,k}}}{2}. \]

    s3 These scores and their disorder laws do not depend on \(\beta\); in both cases the partition function is \(Z_N(\beta)=\sum_\sigma e^{\beta\mathcal E_N(\sigma)}\).

    s4 Let \(\mathcal B_{r,\beta}\) denote the functional (2.11) for the corresponding laws at inverse temperature \(\beta\).

    ▸ Corollary 10.3 (Zero-temperature variational limit)¶

    §10 ¶7¶

    원문

    s1 For each model in Corollaries 10.1 and 10.2, the limit

    \[ g=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\mathbb E\max_\sigma\mathcal E_N(\sigma) \]

    ↳ exists and satisfies

    \[ g=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac1\beta \inf_{r\ge0}\inf_{\zeta,\mathbf m} \mathcal B_{r,\beta}(\zeta,\mathbf m). \tag{10.1} \]

    s2 More precisely, the expression inside the temperature limit lies in \([g,g+\log(2)/\beta]\).

    s3 For unit-weight SAT, the limiting expected minimum number of violated clauses per variable is \(-g\).

    ▸ Proof.¶

    §10 ¶8¶

    원문

    s1 Write \(g_N=N^{-1}\mathbb E\max_\sigma\mathcal E_N(\sigma)\) and \(P_N(\beta)=N^{-1}\mathbb E\log Z_N(\beta)\).

    s2 The first-moment assumptions make \(g_N\) finite and uniformly bounded.

    s3 Since there are \(2^N\) spin configurations,

    \[ g_N\le\frac{P_N(\beta)}\beta \le g_N+\frac{\log2}{\beta}. \]

    s4 For each fixed \(\beta\gt 0\), Theorem 2.1 gives \(P_N(\beta)\to I(\beta)\), where \(I(\beta)=\inf_{r,\zeta,\mathbf m}\mathcal B_{r,\beta}(\zeta,\mathbf m)\).

    s5 Hence

    \[ \limsup_N g_N\le\frac{I(\beta)}\beta \le\liminf_N g_N+\frac{\log2}{\beta}. \]

    s6 Letting \(\beta\to\infty\) proves that \(g_N\) converges to a limit \(g\).

    s7 Taking \(N\to\infty\) in the finite-volume bounds now gives \(g\le I(\beta)/\beta\le g+\log(2)/\beta\), proving (10.1).

    s8 In the satisfiability model, maximizing the negative violation count is the same as minimizing that count.
