    §7.1 Averages over depths¶

    §7.1 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 A topology is a finite rooted tree on labeled leaves, with unary vertices suppressed.

    s2 Each branching vertex is assigned an integer depth in \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\), increasing strictly along every branch.

    s3 Write \(b(S)\) for the number of branching vertices of a topology \(S\).

    s4 Fix \(\eta\gt 0\).

    s5 A depth assignment is \(\eta\)-regular if every assigned depth divided by \(L\) lies in \((\eta,1-\eta)\) and the normalized depths of distinct branching vertices differ by more than \(\eta\).

    s6 When an additional prefix depth \(d\) is specified, it must satisfy the same restrictions relative to all branching depths.

    s7 When two copies of one topology use aligned depths, corresponding vertices share one depth coordinate; regularity is imposed on these distinct coordinates.

    §7.1 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 For a nonnegative array \(z(\mathbf q)\) on \(a\) depth coordinates, put

    \[ \mathcal A_{L,\eta}z =L^{-a}\sum_{\mathbf q\;\eta\text{-regular}}z(\mathbf q). \tag{7.1} \]

    s2 Additional ordering restrictions, such as \(d\lt \min_v q_v\), are included in the summation domain.

    s3 These are unnormalized averages: their total mass is at most one.

    s4 Consequently, for \(0\lt \gamma\le1\),

    \[ \mathcal A_{L,\eta}(z^\gamma) \le (\mathcal A_{L,\eta}z)^\gamma. \tag{7.2} \]

    s5 Two elementary comparisons will recur.

    s6 Summing over an unused coordinate costs at most \(L\), canceled by its normalization.

    s7 A fixed integer shift of any subset of the coordinates maps \(\eta\)-regular assignments injectively into \(\eta/2\)-regular assignments for all sufficiently large \(L\).

    §7.2 Conditional covariance and continuity in depth¶

    §7.2 ¶1¶

    원문

    s1 For a sampled copy of \(S\), set

    \[ A_S=\left(\prod_{a\in S}\sigma_i^a\right)_{i\le N}, \qquad R_S=\mathbf1\cdot A_S, \qquad x\cdot y=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Nx_i y_i, \qquad \|x\|_N^2=x\cdot x. \]

    s2 At a depth \(d\) on its initial stem, including its first branching depth, let \(\mathscr F_d\) be generated by the root and the sampled prefix through \(d\), and define

    \[ \begin{align*} \mu_{d,S}&=\mathbb E[A_S\mid\mathscr F_d], \\ v_N(S,d)&=\frac1{N^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i,j=1}^N \operatorname{Cov}(A_{S,i},A_{S,j}\mid\mathscr F_d)^2. \tag{7.5} \end{align*} \]

    s3 The expectation includes the root.

    s4 Every coordinate of \(A_S\) and \(\mu_{d,S}\) has absolute value at most one, and \(0\le v_N(S,d)\le1\).

    s5 The next lemma explains why this squared covariance is the useful quantity to induct on.

    ▸ Lemma 7.2 (Contractions and three copies)¶

    §7.2 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 If a random vector \(B\in[-1,1]^N\) is conditionally independent of \(A_S\) given \(\mathscr F_d\), then

    \[ \bigl\|(A_S-\mu_{d,S})\cdot B\bigr\|_{L^2} \le v_N(S,d)^{1/4}. \tag{7.6} \]

    s2 If \(X,Y,Z\) are conditionally independent copies of a bounded random vector given a sigma field \(\mathscr G\), and \(C\) is its conditional covariance matrix, then

    \[ \mathbb E(X\cdot Y-X\cdot Z)^2 \ge \frac2{N^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}^2. \tag{7.7} \]

    ▸ Proof.¶

    §7.2 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 Conditional on \(\mathscr F_d\) and \(B\), the squared contraction has expectation \(N^{-2}\sum_{ij}C_{ij}B_iB_j\).

    s2 Its absolute value is at most \(N^{-2}\sum_{ij}|C_{ij}|\).

    s3 Cauchy–Schwarz, first over \(i,j\) and then over the prefix, proves (7.6).

    s4 For the second assertion, write \(\mu=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathscr G]\).

    s5 Conditional independence gives

    \[ \mathbb E[(X\cdot Y-X\cdot Z)^2\mid\mathscr G] =\frac2{N^2}\operatorname{tr}\bigl((C+\mu\mu^{\mathsf T})C\bigr) \ge\frac2{N^2}\operatorname{tr}(C^2), \]

    ↳ since \(C\) is positive semidefinite.

    ▸ Lemma 7.3 (Averaged continuity in depth)¶

    §7.2 ¶4¶

    원문

    s1 Fix a topology \(S\) and a subset \(U\) of its branching vertices.

    s2 For each \(\eta\)-regular depth assignment, let \(S^U\) have the same topology with the depths of the vertices in \(U\) increased by one.

    s3 At an ancestral evaluation depth \(d\), compare the means using the same sampled prefix.

    s4 Then, for all sufficiently large \(L\),

    \[ \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\mathbb E \|\mu_{d,S^U}-\mu_{d,S}\|_N^2\le C/L. \tag{7.8} \]

    s5 The average ranges over all branching depths; the coordinate \(d\) is also averaged if it ranges, and otherwise is held fixed.

    s6 If \(d\) ranges along an initial unary stretch of \(S\), then

    \[ \mathcal A_{L,\eta}\mathbb E \|\mu_{d+1,S}-\mu_{d,S}\|_N^2\le C/L, \tag{7.9} \]

    ↳ where this average includes \(d\) and all branching depths, and the means are evaluated along the same sampled path.

    s7 The constant depends only on the number of vertices and shifts, uniformly in \(N\), the evaluation depth, and the transition kernels.

    §7.2 ¶5¶

    원문

    s1 Keep the later branching depths of \(T\) fixed and sample a single path along its initial stem.

    s2 On this path, \(\mu_{t,T}\) is a bounded vector martingale.

    s3 Orthogonality of its increments gives

    \[ \sum_t\mathbb E\|\mu_{t+1,T}-\mu_{t,T}\|_N^2\le1. \tag{7.10} \]

    s4 This proves the evaluation-depth assertion after summing over the ranged depth and dividing by \(L\).

    식 (7.10) 항별 표

    §7.2 ¶6¶

    원문

    s1 Consider a branching vertex at depth \(q\) with child shapes \(T_1,\ldots,T_s\).

    s2 Holding their later depths fixed, its original conditional mean is \(\prod_j \mu_{q,T_j}\), with products understood coordinatewise.

    s3 If the split is delayed to \(q+1\), its conditional mean at the same prefix of depth \(q\) is \(\mathbb E[\prod_j \mu_{q+1,T_j}\mid\mathscr F_q]\), where the child means are evaluated on a common one-step continuation.

    s4 Jensen's inequality and the product difference inequality on \([-1,1]^s\) yield

    \[ \mathbb E\left\|\prod_j \mu_{q,T_j} -\mathbb E\left[\prod_j \mu_{q+1,T_j}\mid\mathscr F_q\right] \right\|_N^2 \le s\sum_j\mathbb E\|\mu_{q+1,T_j}-\mu_{q,T_j}\|_N^2. \tag{7.11} \]

    s5 Summing over \(q\) uses (7.10) with each child shape fixed.

    s6 Propagating a changed subtree mean towards an ancestral evaluation consists of conditional expectations and multiplication by factors bounded by one.

    s7 Both operations contract this squared norm.

    s8 Averaging over all other depth coordinates therefore proves the claimed \(C/L\) bound for one shift.

    s9 For several shifts, change the vertices one at a time and use the squared triangle inequality with a factor equal to the number of shifts.

    s10 Regularity ensures that the intermediate shapes have the same ancestry relations and, for large \(L\), lie in the enlarged \(\eta/2\)-regular domain.

    s11 All relevant one-prefix marginal laws are unchanged when other sampled branches are omitted, so these estimates hold in any larger sampled tree.

    §7.2 ¶7¶

    원문

    s1 Suppose now that \(S\) first branches at depth \(e\) into proper child shapes \(T_1,\ldots,T_s\).

    s2 Conditional independence of the children gives \(\mu_{e,S}=\prod_j\mu_{e,T_j}\).

    s3 Write

    \[ a_{S,e}=\prod_{j=1}^s \mu_{e,T_j}, \qquad p_N(S,e)=\sum_{j=1}^s v_N(T_j,e)^{1/4}. \tag{7.12} \]

    s4 For any vector \(B\in[-1,1]^N\) independent of the continuation of \(S\) given its prefix at \(e\), successive replacement of the children, using (7.6), gives

    \[ \|(A_S-a_{S,e})\cdot B\|_{L^2}\le p_N(S,e). \tag{7.13} \]

    s5 At each replacement the other child vectors and the already replaced means form a bounded multiplier independent of the child being replaced, conditionally on \(\mathscr F_e\).

    s6 In particular this applies to \(B=\mathbf1\), and to a vector sampled on another side separated before \(e\).

    s7 For two such sides, both can be projected with total \(L^2\) error at most \(2p_N(S,e)\).

    s8 Multiplication of the contracted error by any scalar test \(f\) with \(|f|\le1\), even a correlated one, does not increase its \(L^1\) bound.

    ▸ Proof.¶

    §7.3 ¶2¶

    원문

    s1 We induct on the number \(k\) of leaves.

    s2 The induction replaces each proper child by its conditional mean.

    s3 It then applies the prescribed-tree identity to two copies with delayed branching depths; after summing the signed coefficients, this becomes a nonnegative three-copy estimate.

    s4 To keep track of the depth averages, let

    \[ V_{k,L}(\eta)=\max_{1\le |S|\le k} \limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal A_{L,\eta}v_N(S,d). \tag{7.15} \]

    s5 The maximum is over finitely many labeled topologies.

    s6 Averages of any lower-order covariance used below, including averages with extra unused coordinates or shifted depths, are bounded by the appropriate \(V_{k-1,L}(\eta/2)\), using (7.2) when fractional powers occur.

    §7.3 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 One leaf.

    s2 Take two old paths splitting at \(d\), with path \(1\) a spin anchor, and add one color required to split from the anchor exactly at \(d\).

    s3 The available old path \(2\) has coefficient \(1\).

    s4 A fresh path can split from both old paths at \(d\), with coefficient \(-c_d\), or follow path \(2\) and leave it at \(\ell\gt d\), with coefficient \(-c_\ell\), where

    \[ c_d=2m_{d+1}-m_d=(d+2)\delta, \qquad c_\ell=\delta\quad(d\lt \ell\le L-1). \tag{7.16} \]

    s5 From the anchor alone the total coefficient is \(-\delta\).

    s6 Write \(X=R_{12}\) and \(X_\ell=R_{13}\) for the corresponding fresh extension.

    s7 Each \(X_\ell\) has the marginal law of \(X\), and \(\sum_{\ell=d}^{L-1}c_\ell=1+\delta\).

    s8 The prescribed-tree identity with \(f=X\) therefore gives

    \[ \frac12\sum_{\ell=d}^{L-1}c_\ell \mathbb E(X-X_\ell)^2 =\delta\operatorname{Var}(X)+o_N(1) \le\delta+o_N(1). \tag{7.17} \]

    s9 For \(\ell=d\), the three spin vectors are conditionally independent below \(d\).

    s10 Hence (7.7) and \(c_d\ge\eta\) give \(v_N(S,d)\le\delta/\eta+o_N(1)\) at each fixed \(L,d\).

    s11 This proves \(V_{1,L}(\eta)\le\delta/\eta\).

    §7.3 ¶6¶

    원문

    s1 Projecting the two target sides.

    s2 In every extension, the marginal target tree consists of two ordinary copies of \(S^+\), independent below their separation at \(d\).

    s3 By (7.13) its multioverlap differs in \(L^2\) from the contraction of its projected means at \(e+1\) by at most \(2p_N(S^+,e+1)\).

    s4 Change the proper child depths back by one, then change their evaluation depth from \(e+1\) to \(e\).

    s5 The product difference inequality and Lemma 7.3 show that this adds an error at most \(h_N(S)\) in \(L^2\), with

    \[ \mathcal A_{L,\eta}h_N(S)^2\le C_k/L. \tag{7.21} \]

    s6 One can take \(h_N(S)\) to be the sum of the finitely many \(L^2(\|\cdot\|_N)\) distances in these comparisons, multiplied by a size-dependent constant.

    s7 This definition depends only on the single-copy marginal shapes, not on the particular extension.

    s8 Thus (7.21) holds uniformly for the comparisons in the extension sum, even though the union of the old and target trees itself need not have regular depths.

    §7.3 ¶9¶

    원문

    s1 For a fixed extension history, retain every old path and the first target-II prefix through \(e\), and delete all other new branches.

    s2 The target-I projection \(a\) already depends only on the retained old anchor prefix.

    s3 Each deleted subtree integrates to one under its probability kernels; a reused old leaf introduces no new variable.

    s4 The retained tree depends only on the class: it is the old tree with a fresh stem attached at \(\ell\lt e\), or just the old tree in the remaining class.

    s5 The history is a combinatorial choice, not a condition on sampled values.

    s6 Thus the joint marginal of the old paths and projected prefixes is fixed within each class.

    s7 In particular, the expectation of \(f\) times the projected overlap is constant within a class and can be multiplied by its total signed coefficient.

    §7.3 ¶12¶

    원문

    s1 Closing the induction.

    s2 Equation (7.13) gives \(\|f-a\cdot b\|_{L^2}\le2p_N(S,e)\).

    s3 Moreover \(\sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell=(e+1)\delta\le1\).

    s4 Replacing \(f\) by \(a\cdot b\) on the left of (7.22) therefore costs at most \(4p_N(S,e)\).

    s5 Each \(a\cdot b_\ell\) has the same marginal law as \(a\cdot b\), so that left side becomes

    \[ \frac12\sum_{\ell=d}^{e-1}c_\ell \mathbb E(a\cdot b-a\cdot b_\ell)^2. \]

    s6 Its summands are nonnegative and \(c_d\ge\eta\).

    s7 The right side of (7.22) is bounded in absolute value by \(2\delta+|\rho_N(S,d)|\).

    s8 Hence

    \[ \mathbb E(a\cdot b-a\cdot b_d)^2 \le C_\eta\bigl(\delta+p_N(S,e)+|\rho_N(S,d)|\bigr). \tag{7.24} \]

    s9 Sample three copies of \(A_S\) conditionally independently below \(d\).

    s10 Projecting each of the two contractions involving the first copy changes it in \(L^2\) by at most \(2p_N(S,e)\).

    s11 Using (7.7), (7.24), and \(p_N(S,e)\le k\), we obtain

    \[ v_N(S,d)\le C_{k,\eta}\bigl( \delta+p_N(S,e)+p_N(S^+,e+1)+h_N(S)\bigr) +C_{L,k,\eta}\varepsilon_{N,L,k}. \tag{7.25} \]

    s12 Each child in either \(p_N\) has fewer than \(k\) leaves.

    s13 To make the averaging step explicit, the aligned construction has \(D=b(S)+1\) distinct depth coordinates, including \(d\) and \(e\).

    s14 A term belonging to \(T_j\) uses only \(D_j=b(T_j)+1\) coordinates.

    s15 For the shifted term these are \(\pi_j(\mathbf q)=(e+1,(q_v+1)_{v\in\operatorname{Br}(T_j)})\).

    s16 Every retained assignment has at most \(L^{D-D_j}\) preimages, and its coordinates are \(\eta/2\)-regular for large \(L\).

    s17 If \(w_j\) is the child covariance as a function of these coordinates, then, for \(0\lt \gamma\le1\),

    \[ \begin{align*} L^{-D}\sum_{\mathbf q\;\eta\text{-regular}} w_j(\pi_j(\mathbf q))^\gamma &\le L^{-D_j}\sum_{\mathbf y\;\eta/2\text{-regular}}w_j(\mathbf y)^\gamma \\ &\le\left(L^{-D_j}\sum_{\mathbf y\;\eta/2\text{-regular}} w_j(\mathbf y)\right)^\gamma. \tag{7.26} \end{align*} \]

    s18 The unshifted term has the same bound without translating the coordinates.

    s19 Thus the two aligned copies use single-copy estimates; there is no restriction of an independently averaged pair of copies to a diagonal.

    s20 Taking \(\gamma=1/4\) and then \(\limsup_N\), and using (7.21) and (7.3), gives, for sufficiently large \(L\),

    \[ V_{k,L}(\eta)\le C_{k,\eta}\left( L^{-1/2}+V_{k-1,L}(\eta/2)^{1/4}\right). \tag{7.27} \]

    s21 The maximum over smaller topologies in (7.15) can be absorbed in the right side because these covariances are bounded by one.

    s22 The base case and induction now imply \(V_{k,L}(\eta)\to0\) for every fixed \(k,\eta\), proving the proposition.

    §7.4 ¶3¶

    원문

    s1 Suppose \(S\) first splits at \(e\) and has proper child shapes \(T_1,\ldots,T_s\).

    s2 We will condition one step before \(e\).

    s3 Use \(S\) as the old tree, designate one of its leaves as a marker anchor, and add a full copy of \(S\) all of whose leaves split from the anchor at \(e-1\).
